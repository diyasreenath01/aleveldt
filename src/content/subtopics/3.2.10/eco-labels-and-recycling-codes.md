## Eco-labels and recycling codes

Environmental labels communicate a **particular environmental claim or requirement**. You should recognise the main symbols in the AQA specification and understand what each one tells a designer or consumer.

A label should not automatically be taken to mean that the whole product is sustainable. Different labels consider different parts of a product's environmental impact.

### Mobius Loop

The **Mobius Loop** is the familiar three-arrow recycling symbol. It communicates recycling-related information, such as that a material can be recycled where suitable collection and processing facilities exist.

Its presence does not mean that the material will definitely be recycled. Local facilities, correct disposal and separation of materials still matter.

### Polymer identification codes

Polymer identification codes use a number or abbreviation to identify the **type of polymer**. This helps plastics to be recognised and separated because mixing incompatible polymers can contaminate a recycling stream and reduce the quality of the recycled material.

A polymer code identifies the material; it does **not** guarantee that the product will be accepted by a local recycling scheme.

### European Ecolabel

The **European Ecolabel** is a voluntary environmental labelling scheme for products and services that meet specified environmental criteria. Unlike a label dealing only with energy consumption, its criteria can consider environmental impacts across more than one part of the product life cycle.

For consumers it provides a quick way to identify products that have been assessed against the scheme's criteria. For manufacturers it can help communicate environmental performance and differentiate a product in the market.

### NAPM recycled mark

The **NAPM recycled mark** is associated with paper products and communicates recycled fibre content. It is useful when selecting or specifying recycled paper and board.

### Energy labels

The **EC/EU energy label** allows consumers to compare the energy performance of relevant appliances. Energy labels can influence purchasing decisions because the initial purchase price can be considered alongside energy use during the product's life.

For designers and manufacturers, energy ratings create an incentive to reduce energy consumption where this is an important part of a product's environmental impact.

The AQA specification also names the **Energy Efficient label/logo**, so you should recognise that energy-efficiency marks communicate that a product meets the requirements of the particular scheme.

### Forest Stewardship Council (FSC)

**FSC certification** is used for forest-based materials and products from certified responsible sources and controlled supply chains. The FSC logo is commonly seen on timber, paper, board and packaging.

For a designer, specifying FSC-certified material can provide evidence about the sourcing of forest-based material. It does not by itself prove that the entire product has a low environmental impact.

### EPA ENERGY STAR

**ENERGY STAR** identifies products that meet the programme's energy-efficiency criteria. It is particularly associated with electrical and electronic products such as computers and other equipment.

Like other environmental labels, it addresses a particular issue—energy efficiency—rather than every environmental impact of the product.

### Why eco-labels are useful — and their limitations

Eco-labels can:
- help consumers compare products quickly
- encourage manufacturers to improve environmental performance
- increase consumer confidence when claims are independently assessed
- give manufacturers a way to communicate genuine environmental improvements.

However, environmental information can become **confusing or misleading** when many different labels are used or when consumers do not understand what a symbol actually certifies. Unsupported or exaggerated environmental claims can also contribute to **greenwashing**.

The useful question is therefore: **what exactly does this label certify, and which part of the product's life cycle does it address?**
