## National and international standards

Standards provide agreed requirements, test methods or procedures for products, services and systems. They help improve **safety, quality, reliability, compatibility and efficiency**. Common standards also make trade and manufacturing across different countries easier because designers, manufacturers and suppliers can work to recognised requirements.

### British Standards Institution (BSI)

The **British Standards Institution (BSI)** is the UK's national standards body. It develops British Standards and provides services including product certification, systems assessment and training.

BSI standards can:
- provide consistent requirements for product quality and safety
- reduce waste by making production and distribution more consistent
- give customers greater confidence that recognised requirements have been followed
- help manufacturers improve processes, productivity and reputation.

The **BSI Kitemark** is a certification mark used where a product or service has been independently assessed against the requirements of the relevant scheme. It should not be treated as a general guarantee that every aspect of a product is superior.

### International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

The **ISO** is an independent international organisation made up of national standards bodies. It develops international standards for products, services and systems so organisations in different countries can work to common specifications.

This can improve **quality, safety and efficiency** and make international trade easier. ISO standards are particularly useful where products, components or supply chains operate across several countries.

Two important examples are:
- **ISO 9001** – quality management systems
- **ISO 14001** – environmental management systems.

### BSI and ISO

BSI is the national standards body for the UK, while ISO develops standards internationally. BSI also represents the UK within international standardisation, so British and international standards can be closely connected.

### Benefits and limitations

Working to recognised standards can improve consistency, customer confidence and access to markets. However, certification and compliance may require **testing, documentation, training, monitoring and auditing**, which take time and cost money.

The value therefore depends on the product and market. For safety-critical products or international manufacture, the consistency and confidence created by standards can outweigh the cost of compliance. Meeting a management-system standard also does not mean that every individual product automatically satisfies all safety or environmental legislation.
