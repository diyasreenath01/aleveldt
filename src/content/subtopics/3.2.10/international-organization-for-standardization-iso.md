## International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

The **International Organization for Standardization (ISO)** develops internationally agreed standards for products, services and systems.

International standards provide common specifications and methods that can improve **quality, safety, efficiency and compatibility** and help international trade.

### Why ISO matters in product design

International standards are particularly useful when a product is designed, manufactured, supplied or sold across different countries. They can:
- give designers, suppliers and manufacturers common requirements and terminology
- improve consistency between factories and suppliers
- make components and systems more compatible
- support controlled quality management
- increase confidence between businesses and consumers
- reduce barriers when products and components move through international supply chains.

ISO standards are developed through international collaboration. This is useful because manufacturers and suppliers in different countries can work to recognised common requirements.

### ISO 9001

**ISO 9001** concerns **quality management systems (QMS)**. It focuses on controlled and documented processes, monitoring performance and continual improvement so an organisation can consistently provide products or services that meet requirements.

Possible benefits include:
- more consistent quality and fewer avoidable errors
- clearer procedures and responsibilities
- improved efficiency and customer confidence
- evidence that a recognised quality-management system is being used.

Establishing and maintaining a certified system can require **training, documentation, auditing, monitoring and staff time**, so there is a cost to the organisation. Certification of a management system also does not mean that every individual product is automatically defect-free.

### ISO and BSI

A **British Standard** is a national standard published through BSI, whereas an **ISO standard** is developed internationally. The key point is not that one is automatically better than the other: the designer or manufacturer should use the standard that is **relevant to the product, process and market**.
