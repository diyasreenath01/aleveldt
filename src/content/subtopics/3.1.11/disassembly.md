## Disassembly

**Disassembly** is the systematic separation of a product into components or materials so they can be **maintained, repaired, reused, replaced, recycled or disposed of appropriately**.

Designing for disassembly means considering separation while the product is being designed, rather than trying to solve it only at end of life.

### Cradle-to-cradle design

A **cradle-to-cradle** approach aims to keep materials in useful cycles. Keeping different material types identifiable and separable makes it easier to recover them without contaminating recycling streams.

A permanently bonded mixture of different materials may perform well during use but can be much harder to separate later.

### Releasable fixings

**Polymer snap fittings** can allow fast assembly and, when designed to be releasable, can also allow components to be separated without destroying them.

**Standard fasteners**, such as Pozidriv screws, can be removed with commonly available tools. This supports repair and component replacement as well as end-of-life disassembly.

The fixing method therefore affects more than assembly speed: permanent adhesives or welded joints may simplify manufacture but can make later separation difficult.

### End-of-life requirements

Products such as vehicles contain many materials and components that need to be recovered or treated at end of life. Designing with **End of Life Vehicles (ELV)** requirements in mind encourages easier removal and separation of components and materials for recovery and recycling.

### Active disassembly

The notes also identify **active disassembly**, where smart materials such as **shape memory alloys (SMA) or shape memory polymers (SMP)** can be used so a fixing or component changes in response to a stimulus, allowing parts to separate automatically or with less manual work.

This can make separation more efficient, but it adds design complexity and is only worthwhile where the recovery benefit justifies it.

### Design judgement

Good design for disassembly balances **manufacturing speed, product strength, safety, appearance, maintenance and end-of-life recovery**. A permanent joint may be appropriate where separation would create a safety risk, while a serviceable or recyclable product benefits from accessible, releasable and standardised connections.
