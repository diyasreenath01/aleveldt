## 2D and 3D drawing

Designers use different drawing methods for different purposes. A good answer should explain **what information the drawing communicates and who needs it**.

### 2D drawing

2D drawings are useful when accurate shape, dimensions and manufacturing information are needed. Orthographic views can show the front, plan and side of a product without perspective distortion. Sections can reveal internal details that would otherwise be hidden.

2D CAD is widely used for working drawings because dimensions, geometry and tolerances can be edited accurately.

### 3D drawing

3D drawings help clients and users understand the overall form of a product. Useful methods include isometric drawing, perspective drawing and rendered CAD models.

3D CAD can show form, materials, colour and surface finish and can be viewed from different angles. It may also be used to create exploded views showing how components fit together.

### Rendering and mixed media

Rendering adds visual information such as colour, texture, highlights, shadows and material appearance. Mixed media can combine sketches, photographs, digital drawing and annotation to communicate a proposal quickly.

### Choosing the right communication method

A presentation image for a client may prioritise realistic appearance, while a drawing for a manufacturer should prioritise **dimensions, tolerances, materials and construction details**. No single drawing style is best for every purpose.
