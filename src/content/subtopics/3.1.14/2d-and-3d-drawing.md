## 2D and 3D drawing

Designers choose drawing methods according to the information that must be communicated and the intended audience.

### 2D drawing

**Orthographic projection** communicates accurate manufacturing information through separate front, plan and side views. **Third-angle projection** is a standard convention used to position these views. **Sectional drawings** reveal internal features that would otherwise be hidden.

2D CAD is widely used for working drawings because dimensions, geometry, line types and tolerances can be edited accurately.

### 3D drawing

- **Isometric drawing** uses axes at 30° to the horizontal and is useful for clear 3D technical sketches.
- **One-point perspective** gives a realistic view where receding lines converge towards one vanishing point.
- **Two-point perspective** uses two vanishing points and is useful for showing products or spaces from an angled viewpoint.
- **Oblique/crating techniques** can help construct proportioned 3D forms quickly during idea development.

### Rendering and mixed media

Rendering communicates material and surface qualities using colour, texture, highlights, shadows and grain. Marker rendering, coloured pencils, digital media and mixed-media drawings can all be used depending on the speed and realism required.

### Choosing the communication method

A client presentation may prioritise realistic form, colour and finish, while a manufacturer needs dimensions, tolerances, materials and construction details. The most effective drawing is therefore the one that communicates the required information clearly, rather than the most visually impressive one.
