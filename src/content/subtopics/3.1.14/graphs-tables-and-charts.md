## Graphs, tables and charts

Graphs, tables and charts help designers communicate **market research, user preferences, testing results and comparisons** clearly.

### Choosing the right format

- **Table:** best for showing exact values and comparing several variables.
- **Bar chart:** useful for comparing separate categories, such as preferred materials or product concepts.
- **Line graph:** useful for showing change over time or continuous data, such as temperature during a test.
- **Pie chart:** can show proportions of a whole, although a bar chart is often easier when accurate comparison matters.
- **Scatter graph:** useful when investigating whether two variables may be related.

### Presenting data properly

Use a clear title, labelled axes, units and a sensible scale. Do not distort the scale to exaggerate differences. Where relevant, state the sample size because a result from 10 users is less representative than a well-selected larger sample.

### The important design skill

Do not stop at presenting the graph. **Interpret it and make a design decision.**

For example:

**70% of target users preferred the larger grip → increase grip diameter in the next prototype → test whether comfort improves without making the product too bulky.**

This turns data into evidence for iterative design.
