## The importance of accuracy

**Accuracy** describes how close a manufactured dimension is to its required value. Accuracy matters at every scale of production because components must fit together correctly and products must function safely and consistently.

A **tolerance** is the permitted variation from a specified dimension. For example, a component specified as **50 ± 0.5 mm** is acceptable from **49.5 mm to 50.5 mm**.

Tighter tolerances may be required for interchangeable, mating or moving parts, although achieving them can increase manufacturing time and cost.

### Ways to improve accuracy

- A **datum** provides a common reference edge or surface and reduces cumulative measurement error.
- A **template** gives a repeatable shape or marking pattern.
- A **jig** holds or locates work and guides a tool.
- A **fixture** holds work securely in the correct position.
- **Vernier calipers and micrometers** provide more precise measurements than a standard ruler.
- **CNC equipment** follows programmed movements to manufacture repeated parts consistently.

Jigs, fixtures and templates are particularly valuable in batch and mass production because an uncontrolled error could otherwise be repeated across many products.
