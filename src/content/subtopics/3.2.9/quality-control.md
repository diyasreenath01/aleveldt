<section class="note-panel"><h2>Quality Control</h2><div class="note-intro"><p><strong>Quality control (QC)</strong> involves <strong>monitoring, measuring and testing</strong> materials or products to detect defects. This can include dimensional checks, inspection of finishes and testing production samples.</p><p><strong>QA plans for quality; QC checks whether the required quality has been achieved.</strong></p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>QC in Production</h3><p>Quality control can use dimensional checks, inspection of finishes, production samples, go/no-go gauges, CMM measurements and other tests appropriate to the product and the required level of accuracy.</p></div></section>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Non-destructive testing</h3><p>AQA specifically includes <strong>X-rays and ultrasound</strong> as non-destructive testing methods. They can be used to inspect components for internal defects without destroying the part, which is valuable where a component is expensive or must remain usable after inspection.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Digital and rapid checking</h3><p><strong>Go/no-go gauges</strong> provide a rapid pass/fail check against dimensional limits. <strong>Laser or probe scanning</strong> can check manufactured geometry, while digital measuring devices such as <strong>vernier calipers and micrometers</strong> provide accurate dimensional measurements.</p></div>
