## Quality control (QC)

**Quality control (QC)** checks the quality of **materials, components and products during manufacture and on completion**. Inspection, measurement and testing compare results with the specification and identify items that pass or fail.

**QA plans and manages processes to prevent defects; QC checks actual outputs to detect defects.**

### Inspection methods

- **Visual inspection and touch:** look or feel for scratches, cracks, poor assembly, surface damage and finish defects.
- **Colour matching:** compare the product with an approved colour reference to identify variation between batches.
- **Chemical analysis or sample testing:** check material composition or another specified property when it cannot be confirmed by appearance alone.
- **Dimensional checking:** use **digital vernier calipers** for external, internal or depth measurements and **micrometers** for small dimensions requiring greater precision. Compare readings with the **specified tolerance**, not simply the nominal size.
- **Interval sampling:** inspect products at defined points in a run, such as every hundredth item, to monitor whether the process remains within limits.

### Inspection levels

| Level | What happens | Main consideration |
|---|---|---|
| **100% inspection** | Every item or specified feature is checked. | More thorough coverage, but slower and more expensive; inspection itself is not infallible. |
| **Normal inspection (sampling)** | A defined sample is inspected from a batch or at intervals. | Reduces checking time, but an unchecked item could still be defective. |
| **Reduced inspection** | A smaller or less frequent sample is checked when a consistently reliable process justifies it. | Saves resources, but inspection should increase if quality becomes less reliable. |

The level depends on the risk of failure, the number of products, process history and inspection cost.

### Destructive versus non-destructive testing

| Destructive testing | Non-destructive testing (NDT) |
|---|---|
| Tests a specimen to failure or leaves it unusable to establish its limits. | Checks an item without damaging it so it can still be used or sold. |
| Examples: a **tensile test to fracture** or an **impact test** that breaks a sample. | Examples: **visual inspection, X-rays** to reveal internal flaws and **ultrasound** to detect internal discontinuities. |
| Usually performed on **representative samples**, because the tested pieces are sacrificed. | May be used on **samples or every item**, including high-value or safety-critical components. |

Testing under **controlled laboratory conditions**, following a consistent procedure, helps make results reliable and comparable with specification limits.

### Go/no-go gauges

A **go/no-go gauge** checks whether a feature is inside its permitted dimensional limits **without giving a numerical reading**.

1. The **GO** end represents the limit that **must fit or pass**.
2. The **NO-GO** end represents the limit that **must not fit or pass**.
3. If GO passes and NO-GO does not, the feature passes the gauge check. Otherwise it fails.

This is quick for repeated production checks, but it does not show the actual dimension or how close the part is to either limit.

### Coordinate measuring machines (CMMs)

A **coordinate measuring machine (CMM)** uses a probe to record points on a component and calculate **sizes, distances, positions and geometric features**. Measurements can be compared with a CAD model or drawing to check tolerances. The collected geometry can also support **reverse engineering** into a 3D CAD model.

CMMs are useful for precise and repeated inspection of complex parts, but the achievable accuracy depends on the machine, setup, environment and calibration; **no single tolerance applies to every CMM**.

### Acting on QC results

A failed product may be **rejected, reworked or investigated**. Recording the type and frequency of faults helps identify recurring issues, such as worn tools or incorrect machine settings, so the production process and QA procedures can be improved. **QC finds defects; QA helps prevent their recurrence.**
