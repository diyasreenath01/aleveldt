<section class="note-panel"><h2>Quality Control</h2><div class="note-intro"><p><strong>Quality control (QC)</strong> involves <strong>monitoring, checking, measuring and testing</strong> materials, components, equipment and products throughout production to detect defects and ensure they conform to specified standards and acceptable tolerances.</p><p><strong>QA plans for quality; QC checks whether the required quality has been achieved.</strong> QC therefore focuses on the actual output of the manufacturing process.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>QC throughout production</h3><p>Quality control should not only happen at the end. Raw materials and bought-in components can be checked before production, dimensions and finishes can be checked during manufacture, and completed products can be inspected or tested before dispatch.</p><p>A <strong>tolerance</strong> is the permitted variation from a specified dimension or value. QC determines whether a component is within that acceptable range.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Go/no-go gauges</h3><p>A <strong>go/no-go gauge</strong> quickly checks whether a manufactured feature lies within its permitted limits. The ‘go’ part should fit and the ‘no-go’ part should not. This is useful in repetitive batch or mass production because it is faster than repeatedly taking a numerical measurement.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Digital and precision measuring</h3><p><strong>Digital vernier callipers</strong> can accurately measure internal, external and depth dimensions. <strong>Micrometers</strong> are used for very precise measurements such as material thickness or shaft diameter. Laser or probe scanning can also compare manufactured components with the required dimensions or digital model.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Non-destructive testing</h3><p><strong>Non-destructive testing (NDT)</strong> checks a material or component for defects without damaging it. AQA examples include <strong>X-rays</strong> and <strong>ultrasound</strong>, which can reveal internal faults that may not be visible from the surface.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>QA vs QC — exam distinction</h3><p><strong>QA is preventative:</strong> it controls the process to reduce the chance of faults occurring.<br><strong>QC is detective:</strong> it measures, inspects and tests to identify whether the required quality has actually been achieved.</p></div></section>
