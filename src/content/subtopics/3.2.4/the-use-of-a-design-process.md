## The use of a design process

A **design process** is the organised development of a solution from an initial problem to a tested product. It is usually iterative, meaning designers return to earlier stages when research, modelling or testing shows that something needs to change.

A typical process includes:

1. Identifying the problem and writing a design brief
2. Researching users, existing products and materials
3. Writing a design specification
4. Generating and developing ideas
5. Modelling and making prototypes
6. Testing, evaluating and refining the design
7. Planning manufacture

### Investigations and analysis

A **design brief** describes the problem, intended users and purpose without prescribing the final solution. Research may involve observing users, interviewing clients, examining and disassembling competing products, testing materials and analysing the intended environment. Findings should influence later design decisions.

### Design specification

A **design specification** lists requirements the product should meet, such as **function, target user, size, materials, ergonomics, safety, appearance, cost, maintenance and environmental impact**.

Requirements should be measurable where possible. “The box must hold six tools securely” is more useful than “the box must have plenty of space”.

### Inspiration and idea generation

Designers can use **mood boards and other inspiration materials**, sketches, models and discussion to explore ideas.

**SCAMPER** prompts designers to **Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate and Reverse** aspects of an idea.

### Modelling, planning, testing and evaluation

**Sketch models** quickly explore ideas, **scale models** communicate proportions and appearance, and **full-size models** are useful for testing ergonomics and function.

Planning identifies materials, equipment, operations and quality checks. Evaluation should continue throughout development: designers test concepts, gather feedback, identify problems and explain how the evidence affects the next version.
