<section class="note-panel"><h2>New Manufacturing Methods</h2><div class="study-card"><h3>Electrohydraulic Forming</h3><p>An electrical discharge creates a shockwave in water, forcing sheet metal against a former. This can form detailed shapes quickly, including <strong>car parts and appliance casings</strong>.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Metal Additive Manufacturing</h3><p>A laser fuses metal powder <strong>layer by layer</strong> to build a part from a digital model. This can produce complex shapes and internal channels that would be difficult to machine. It is useful for <strong>prototypes and customised parts</strong>, although finishing may still be required.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Fibre Injection Moulding</h3><p>A polymer mixed with reinforcing fibres is injected into a mould. The fibres can improve the <strong>strength and stiffness</strong> of lightweight parts used in products such as vehicles and sports equipment.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Laser Beam Welding</h3><p>A concentrated laser joins metal with a <strong>narrow, precise weld</strong>. It is useful for thin components and can cause less distortion than a wider heat source.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Physical Vapour Deposition</h3><p><strong>PVD</strong> deposits a thin coating onto a product’s surface. The coating can improve <strong>wear resistance or appearance</strong>, for example on cutting tools or decorative products.</p></div></section>
