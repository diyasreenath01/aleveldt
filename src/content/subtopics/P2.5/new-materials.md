<section class="note-panel"><h2>New Materials</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>The choice of material should be explained through a link between its <strong>properties</strong> and its <strong>use</strong>.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Glulam</h3><p>Layers of bonded timber form strong, lightweight beams that can be curved. Typical uses include buildings and bridges with large spans.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> Glulam can be formed into a curved roof beam. Bonding several timber layers together helps produce the required shape while distributing defects found in individual pieces of wood.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Kevlar</h3><p>Strong, tough and lightweight fibre. Used for protective equipment, puncture resistant tyres and aircraft components.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Graphene</h3><p>Extremely thin carbon sheet with high tensile strength and good electrical and thermal conductivity. Potential uses include sensors, electronics, coatings and batteries.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Precious metal clay</h3><p>Mouldable metal particles that form a solid metal object after firing. Used for jewellery and decorative products.</p></div></section>
