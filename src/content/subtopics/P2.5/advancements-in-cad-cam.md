<section class="note-panel"><h2>How Technology Changed Design and Manufacture</h2><div class="note-intro"><p><strong>Computer aided design (CAD)</strong> lets designers create, edit and test digital models. <strong>Computer aided manufacture (CAM)</strong> uses digital information to control manufacturing equipment, such as CNC machines.</p><p>Digital models can be shared with other designers, modified quickly and tested before a physical prototype is made. In factories, CNC machinery, robotics and digital quality checks can improve accuracy and repeatability. However, digital predictions still need to be checked through physical testing.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Key developments</h3><ul><li><strong>3D printing:</strong> Builds a part layer by layer. It is useful for prototypes, customised parts and complex forms.</li><li><strong>Finite element analysis (FEA):</strong> Predicts how a model may respond to forces or heat, helping designers identify weak areas.</li><li><strong>Computational fluid dynamics (CFD):</strong> Models how liquids or gases flow around or through a design.</li><li><strong>Virtual reality (VR):</strong> Allows designers to experience a digital model at scale and assess features such as layout and ergonomics.</li><li><strong>Cloud based CAD:</strong> Allows people in different locations to work on shared design files.</li></ul></div></section>
