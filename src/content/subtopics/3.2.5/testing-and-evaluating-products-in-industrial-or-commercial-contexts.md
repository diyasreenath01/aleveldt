<section class="note-panel"><h2>Critical Analysis, Testing and Feedback</h2><div class="note-intro"><p><strong>Critical analysis</strong> is an objective study of a design's strengths and weaknesses. It considers its purpose, users, materials, manufacture, usability and safety.</p><p><strong>Evaluation</strong> judges how well a design meets its specification. It should use evidence from testing. For example, if users struggle to open a box, the designer should identify why and test a different handle or opening mechanism.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Product Testing</h3><p>Commercial products are tested before sale to check <strong>function, safety, durability and performance</strong>. Tests should reflect conditions of use. Failure to identify a serious fault can lead to a <strong>product recall</strong>, where products are withdrawn or returned for correction.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Destructive Testing</h3><p><strong>Destructive testing</strong> pushes a sample until it breaks or is permanently damaged. It reveals limits such as maximum load but makes the sample unusable.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Non-destructive Testing</h3><p><strong>Non-destructive testing (NDT)</strong> checks a component for faults without destroying it. Examples include visual inspection and scanning for internal defects. It is useful when a finished part is valuable or must still be used after inspection.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Choosing a Test</h3><p>The choice of test depends on the product and the property being checked. A chair might undergo repeated load testing; an electrical appliance might be checked for overheating.</p></div></section>
