<section class="note-panel"><h2>Polymer enhancement</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>Polymers are enhanced by adding <strong>additives</strong> to the polymer stock, usually while the polymer is molten during processing. These additives improve processing, performance, durability, safety, and environmental behaviour.</p></div>
<div class="note-grid"><div class="note-group-title"><h3>Polymer additives and reasons for adding them</h3></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Lubricants</h4>
<p>Reduce the viscosity of molten polymer, making it less sticky and easier to mould.</p>
<p>Allow more intricate shapes to be formed and enable lower moulding temperatures, reducing energy use.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Thermal antioxidants</h4>
<p>Prevent oxidation of the polymer during high-temperature processing.</p>
<p>Reduce heat-related discolouration and degradation.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Pigments</h4>
<p>Fine particles mixed into molten polymer to provide colour in the final product.</p>
<p>Affect appearance rather than mechanical properties.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Antistatic additives</h4>
<p>Reduce the build-up of static electrical charge on the polymer surface.</p>
<p>Used where static could cause handling or safety issues.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Flame retardants</h4>
<p>Reduce the likelihood of combustion and slow the spread of fire.</p>
<p>Commonly used in electrical products and vehicle components.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Plasticisers</h4>
<p>Reduce hardness and brittleness at room temperature, increasing flexibility.</p>
<p>Improve processability by allowing polymers to be formed more easily at higher temperatures.</p>
<p>Example: added to LDPE food wrap to allow stretching over food.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Fillers</h4>
<p>Increase bulk so less polymer is required, reducing material cost.</p>
<p>Mineral fillers increase thermal conductivity, allowing faster heating and cooling.</p>
<p>Shorter heating and cooling times result in reduced mould cycle times.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Biodegradable plasticisers</h4>
<p>Make polymers softer and more flexible while increasing ease of breakdown.</p>
<p>Result in faster degradation compared to standard plastics.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Bio-batch additives</h4>
<p>Include oxy-degradable, photo-degradable, and hydro-degradable additives.</p>
<p>Reduce degradation time from hundreds of years to a few years or months.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Antioxidants</h4>
<p>Reduce environmental deterioration caused by oxygen exposure.</p>
<p>Prevent brittleness, surface cracking, and pigment discolouration in outdoor use.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>UV light stabilisers</h4>
<p>Prevent polymer chains breaking down due to ultraviolet radiation.</p>
<p>Reduce colour fading and embrittlement in outdoor products.</p></div></div></section>

<section class="note-panel"><h2>AQA specification link: additives in products</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>AQA specifically requires knowledge of <strong>UV stabilisers</strong> used to prolong polymer life and <strong>bio-batch materials</strong> used to encourage biodegradability. You should also be able to relate additives to specific polymer products.</p></div><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Patio furniture</h3><p>Outdoor polymer products can contain UV stabilisers because prolonged exposure to sunlight can degrade polymers. The stabiliser helps reduce UV-related deterioration and therefore prolongs product life.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Food packaging and carrier bags</h3><p>Additives can be selected to change the required performance or end-of-life behaviour of polymer packaging. AQA specifically identifies food packaging and carrier bags as examples where you should be able to explain the use of additives, including bio-batch materials used to encourage degradation.</p></div></div></section>
