<section class="note-panel"><h2>Testing prototypes</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Prototype testing is an alternative to relying only on computer modelling when assessing feasibility. A prototype allows a proposed design to be tested with potential consumers before full production.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Levels of prototype</h3><p>Testing can range from simple test rigs used to investigate performance through to pre-production prototypes that look and perform like the finished product but are not yet in mass production.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>What can be tested?</h3><p>A simple prototype can test <strong>function and ergonomics</strong> before the design is developed further in relation to production, finish, graphics, packaging and materials.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Why prototype testing helps feasibility</h3><p>Testing with potential consumers gives evidence about whether the design is practical and suitable before committing to production. The feasibility notes use examples such as a coffee-cup carrier, snack-bar packaging and aircraft to prompt consideration of which aspects should be assessed using prototypes.</p></div></section>
