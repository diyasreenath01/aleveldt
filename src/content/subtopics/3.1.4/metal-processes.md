<section class="note-panel"><h2>Metal Processes</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Choose a metal process by considering the <strong>material, shape, accuracy, finish and production quantity</strong>.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Casting</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Sand casting</h3><p>Molten metal is poured into a sand mould formed around a pattern.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> one-off and small-batch components, including large or complex shapes.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> relatively low tooling cost; large components can be produced.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> rougher surface and lower accuracy than die casting; further machining may be required.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Gravity die casting</h3><p>Molten metal flows under gravity into a reusable metal mould.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> repeated batch production of non-ferrous components.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> better accuracy and finish than sand casting; reusable mould.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> higher tooling cost; less economical for very small quantities.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Pressure die casting</h3><p>Molten metal is forced under pressure into a precision metal die.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> high-volume production of detailed components.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> fast, accurate and repeatable with a good surface finish.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> very high die and machine cost, so high quantities are needed.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Investment casting</h3><p>A disposable wax pattern is coated to form a heat-resistant mould; the wax is removed and molten metal fills the cavity.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> complex, accurate components that are difficult to machine.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> fine detail and good dimensional accuracy.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> slower and more expensive than simpler casting methods.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Forming</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Press forming / stamping</h3><p>Sheet metal is cut or formed between a punch and die using a press.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> repeated sheet-metal parts and high-volume production.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> very fast and repeatable once tooling is made.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> high tooling cost; poor die design can cause tearing or distortion.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Deep drawing</h3><p>A punch draws sheet metal into a die to create a deep hollow form.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> cans, sinks and other seamless hollow products.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> strong seamless forms with little joining.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> requires suitable ductile sheet and careful control to avoid wrinkling or tearing.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Rolling</h3><p>Metal passes between rollers to reduce thickness or create sheet, strip or sections.</p><ul><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> continuous, consistent production.</li><li><strong>Limitation:</strong> large industrial equipment is required.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Forging</h3><p>Metal is shaped by compressive force, often while hot.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> highly stressed components such as tools and mechanical parts.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> produces strong components because the grain flow follows the shape.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> specialist dies/equipment and significant force are required.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Machining and cutting</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Turning</h3><p>The workpiece rotates on a lathe while a cutting tool removes material.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> cylindrical parts, shafts and threaded features.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> accurate diameters and good surface finish.</li><li><strong>Limitation:</strong> mainly suited to rotational forms.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Milling</h3><p>A rotating multi-point cutter removes material from a fixed workpiece.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> slots, faces, pockets and complex profiles.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> versatile and accurate; CNC milling gives high repeatability.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> material is wasted as swarf and complex work may need several setups.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Drilling</h3><p>A rotating drill produces circular holes. Hole position, diameter and depth must be controlled where components must fit accurately.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Laser cutting</h3><p>A CNC-controlled laser cuts sheet metal directly from digital data.</p><ul><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> accurate, repeatable and ideal for complex profiles or quick design changes.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> equipment and machine time are expensive; heat can affect cut edges.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Joining</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Welding</h3><p>Heat fuses metal components to create a permanent joint.</p><ul><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> strong permanent joint with no separate fastener.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> heat can distort material; skill, ventilation and suitable PPE are required.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Brazing and soldering</h3><p>A filler metal melts and flows into the joint while the parent metals remain solid. Brazing uses a higher-temperature filler than soldering.</p><ul><li><strong>Useful when:</strong> joining thin components or dissimilar metals without melting the parent material.</li><li><strong>Limitation:</strong> strength and temperature resistance depend on filler and joint design.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Mechanical fixings</h3><p>Bolts, screws and rivets join components without fusion.</p><ul><li><strong>Bolts/screws:</strong> useful where dismantling or maintenance is required.</li><li><strong>Rivets:</strong> quick permanent fixings for sheet material.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Choosing a metal process</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Consider <strong>shape, quantity, tolerance, finish, tooling cost, speed and waste</strong>. Expensive die processes become economical at high volumes, while machining or sand casting can suit lower quantities.</p></div></section>
