<section class="note-panel"><h2>Adhesives and fixings</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>For AQA 7552 you should know the specified adhesives, what materials they are suitable for, and how they are applied.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Polyvinyl acetate (PVA)</h3><ul><li>Water-based adhesive suited to porous materials, particularly woods.</li><li>Not suitable for non-porous plastics and metals.</li><li>Dries clear.</li><li>Surfaces should be slightly rough and clean before application.</li><li>Wipe away excess glue because overflow can stain.</li><li>Pressure must be applied while it dries.</li><li>Drying time in these notes: approximately 4–14 hours.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Epoxy resin</h3><ul><li>Can bond metals, plastics and woods.</li><li>Can bridge a small gap between two materials.</li><li>Waterproof and suited to relatively small areas.</li><li>Equal amounts of resin and hardener are mixed and applied with a spreader.</li><li>The joint is held together while the adhesive dries.</li><li>Drying time in these notes: approximately ½–6 hours.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Contact adhesive</h3><ul><li>Can be used with metals, plastics and woods and is waterproof.</li><li>Useful for large flat sheets such as laminate flooring.</li><li>Apply adhesive to both surfaces and allow it to become tacky before pressing the surfaces firmly together.</li><li>Once pressed together it bonds immediately, so repositioning is difficult.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>UV hardening adhesive</h3><ul><li>A clear, low-viscosity liquid that cures when exposed to UV light.</li><li>Contains a photoinitiator that causes the adhesive to harden when it absorbs UV radiation.</li><li>The pieces are positioned together, adhesive is placed in the joint, then UV light is applied.</li><li>Can join woods, metals and plastics, but is suited to relatively small areas.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Solvent cement</h3><ul><li>A clear solvent adhesive used for plastics such as acrylic, ABS and PVC.</li><li>The surfaces are placed together without gaps.</li><li>A syringe or small brush can introduce the solvent into the joint by capillary action.</li><li>The solvent softens the surfaces so they join together.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Jigs and fixtures</h3><p>A <strong>jig</strong> helps parts or components to be produced repeatedly, quickly and accurately. It holds work at the correct angle or position and can guide a tool, reducing repeated marking out.</p><p>A <strong>fixture</strong> holds the work in a specific position while a process is carried out, but does not guide the tool.</p></div></section>
