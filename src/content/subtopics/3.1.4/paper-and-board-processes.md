<section class="note-panel"><h2>Paper and Board Processes</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>The teacher revision material focuses on <strong>die cutting and creasing, folding/bending and laser cutting</strong>. Learn the sequence as well as the quality-control and safety points.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Die cutting and creasing</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A steel-rule die cuts a repeated net/profile while creasing rules compress fold lines without cutting through the board.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Select and inspect the sheet for warping or surface damage.</li><li>Position it accurately on the press platen/cutting bed.</li><li>Align the cutting and creasing die with the required registration points.</li><li>Lower the die using the mechanical/hydraulic press.</li><li>Apply controlled force so cutting rules shear through and crease rules form fold lines.</li><li>Remove the component and waste matrix.</li><li>Inspect cut edges, crease depth, dimensions and consistency.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> packaging nets, cartons and repeated card profiles.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> fast and repeatable; cutting and creasing can be completed together.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> dedicated die/tooling cost makes it less suitable for very small quantities.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> accurate registration, sharp cutting rule, correct crease depth, complete cuts and dimensional accuracy.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> cutting and crushing/pinch hazards around the press.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Folding / bending</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Paper/board is folded accurately along a prepared crease line.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Position the sheet accurately.</li><li>Select a suitable creasing rule/wheel for the material thickness.</li><li>Apply controlled pressure to compress the fibres without cutting through.</li><li>Form the crease consistently along the fold line.</li><li>Fold along the crease.</li><li>Inspect alignment and check for cracking or fibre damage.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> boxes, cartons and packaging.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> produces controlled, repeatable folds.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> incorrect crease depth or material choice can crack the surface.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> fold alignment, crease depth/consistency and absence of cracking.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> finger-trap risks on powered creasing/folding equipment.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Laser cutting</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A CNC-controlled laser cuts or etches paper/board directly from digital data.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Secure the sheet flat on the laser bed.</li><li>Load and verify the CAD/CAM file and scale.</li><li>Focus the laser.</li><li>Set power and speed for the material.</li><li>Run the programmed cut/etch path.</li><li>Allow the material to cool and remove it.</li><li>Inspect dimensions, edges and burn marks.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> decorative card, prototypes and customised packaging/forms.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> accurate complex shapes without making a physical cutting die; easy to change digital designs.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> slower/less economical than dedicated die cutting for very high repeated quantities; burning can occur with poor settings.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> secure/flat work, alignment, focus, speed, power, kerf and burn marks.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> extraction, fire risk and never view the laser directly.</p></div></section>
