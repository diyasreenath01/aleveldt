<section class="note-panel"><h2>Polymer Processes</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Select a polymer process according to the <strong>polymer type, product form, accuracy, finish and production quantity</strong>.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Sheet forming</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Vacuum forming</h3><p>A thermoplastic sheet is heated until soft, then atmospheric pressure pulls it over a mould as air is removed.</p><ul><li><strong>Process:</strong> clamp and heat sheet → raise mould → apply vacuum → cool → remove and trim.</li><li><strong>Materials:</strong> HIPS, acrylic and PVC.</li><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> packaging, trays and thin-walled covers.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> relatively low-cost moulds; useful for prototypes and batch production.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> trimming waste; deep shapes can thin; undercuts prevent easy release.</li><li><strong>Mould:</strong> draft angles, rounded corners and air holes improve forming and release.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Thermoforming / matched mould forming</h3><p>A heated thermoplastic sheet is shaped between positive and negative moulds.</p><ul><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> deeper forms and sharper detail than a simple single-mould process.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> more complex tooling and setup.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Line bending</h3><p>A narrow line of thermoplastic sheet is heated until soft and bent around a jig or former.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> one-off and small-batch products with straight bends.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> simple and low-cost.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> overheating can bubble/burn the polymer; only local straight bends are produced.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Moulding</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Injection moulding</h3><p>Molten thermoplastic is injected under pressure into a closed mould.</p><ul><li><strong>Process:</strong> granules enter hopper → rotating screw heats/moves polymer → melt is injected → component cools → part is ejected.</li><li><strong>Materials:</strong> ABS, PP, PS, HDPE and nylon.</li><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> mass-produced detailed components.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> fast, accurate and repeatable; complex features can be moulded in.</li><li><strong>Limitation:</strong> very high mould and setup cost.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Blow moulding</h3><p>A heated hollow tube of thermoplastic (parison) is enclosed in a mould and inflated with air.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> hollow products such as bottles and containers.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> seamless hollow shapes; efficient mass production.</li><li><strong>Limitation:</strong> expensive tooling.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Rotational moulding</h3><p>Polymer powder is placed in a hollow mould which rotates while heated, coating the inside as it melts.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> large seamless hollow products and double-wall forms.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> relatively low-pressure tooling and even wall coverage.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> long cycle time and limited suitable polymers.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Compression moulding</h3><p>A measured charge of polymer is placed in a heated mould and compressed into shape.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> repeated components, particularly thermosets and composites.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> strong dense components and controlled material quantity.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> slower cycle than injection moulding; flash may need trimming.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Continuous processes</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Extrusion</h3><p>Molten polymer is forced continuously through a shaped die to create a constant cross-section.</p><ul><li><strong>Suitable for:</strong> pipes, channels, strips and profiles.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> efficient continuous production and low unit cost at high volume.</li><li><strong>Limitation:</strong> product must have a constant cross-section.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Calendaring</h3><p>Heated polymer passes through rollers to produce sheet or film of controlled thickness.</p><ul><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> continuous production with consistent thickness and surface.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> large equipment and high setup cost.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Composite lay-up</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Lay-up moulding</h3><p>Layers of reinforcement and resin are built up over a prepared mould and allowed to cure.</p><ul><li><strong>Materials:</strong> GRP and CFRP.</li><li><strong>Advantages:</strong> relatively simple tooling; large and complex composite shapes can be produced.</li><li><strong>Limitations:</strong> labour-intensive; trapped air can weaken the laminate; resins need careful handling and ventilation.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Choosing a polymer process</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Link the process to form and scale. <strong>Injection moulding</strong> suits very high quantities because expensive tooling is spread across many units; <strong>vacuum forming</strong> uses cheaper tooling and can suit prototypes or shorter runs of thin-walled products.</p></div></section>
