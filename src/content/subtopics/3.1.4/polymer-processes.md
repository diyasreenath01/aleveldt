<section class="note-panel"><h2>Polymer Processes</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>For each process, know <strong>what it is used for, the production sequence, quality-control points and the main safety risks</strong>. These notes follow the teacher revision slides.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Vacuum forming</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A heated thermoplastic sheet is pulled over a former by a vacuum.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Clamp the thermoplastic sheet securely.</li><li>Heat evenly with radiant heaters until it softens.</li><li>Raise and align the former beneath the sheet.</li><li>Apply vacuum through the air holes so the sheet is drawn over the former.</li><li>Use a plug assist if required to improve thickness distribution.</li><li>Cool while maintaining the vacuum to prevent warping.</li><li>Release, remove and trim the moulding.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> packaging trays, blister packs and yoghurt pots.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> straightforward way to make thin-walled shapes; suitable for repeated sheet products.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> trimming creates waste; deep draws can cause thinning or webbing.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> even temperature; former with tapered sides, rounded edges, smooth finish and air holes; correct suction; check full mould contact, webbing, thinning and dimensions.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> burns from heaters/hot plastic; ventilation.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Thermoforming / pressure forming</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A heated sheet is shaped using a mould with vacuum/pressure and, where required, a plug or plunger.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Heat the thermoplastic sheet evenly.</li><li>Position it accurately over the mould.</li><li>Apply vacuum and/or pressure.</li><li>Use a plug assist if required.</li><li>Cool under controlled conditions.</li><li>Remove, trim and inspect the component.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> containers and trays.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> allows controlled forming and deeper shapes.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> over-drawing can thin the sheet; more involved tooling/setup.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> mould/plug use, registration, consistent thickness, even heating and controlled draw.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> burns; ventilation.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Calendaring</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Heated polymer is squeezed through rollers to produce continuous sheet or film.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Feed heated thermoplastic into the rollers.</li><li>Pass it through a series of heated rollers.</li><li>Control the roller gaps to reduce thickness gradually.</li><li>Form a continuous sheet/film while monitoring the surface.</li><li>Cool to minimise distortion.</li><li>Trim edges, wind onto a roll and inspect.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> PVC sheet and film.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> continuous production with consistent thickness.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> large equipment and careful setup are required.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> roller spacing, lubrication, roller speed, thickness and edge trimming.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> entanglement, crushing, heat and ventilation.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Line bending</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A strip heater softens a narrow line in thermoplastic sheet so it can be bent around a jig.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Align the bend line over the strip heater.</li><li>Heat evenly, on both sides where required.</li><li>Remove and bend around a jig/former.</li><li>Hold in the jig until cool to prevent spring-back.</li><li>Inspect the angle, surface and dimensions.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> displays, signs and fabricated sheet products.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> simple and accurate for straight bends.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> limited to local bend lines; overheating can damage the polymer.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> even heating, correct distance from heater, jig angle, stress whitening/cracking and dimensional accuracy.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> burns.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Compression moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A measured charge is pressed into shape in a heated mould.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Place the measured thermoset charge into the heated mould.</li><li>Close the mould and apply pressure.</li><li>Allow material to flow and fill the cavity.</li><li>Maintain heat and pressure while it cures.</li><li>Open the mould and remove the component.</li><li>Trim flash and inspect.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> electrical fittings and rubber/thermoset mouldings.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> repeatable moulding of thermosetting materials.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> curing takes time; flash may need trimming.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> charge mass, full cavity fill, pressure, cure, voids and surface finish.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot mould, crushing and ventilation.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Lamination / GRP lay-up</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Resin and reinforcement are built up over a mould to create a composite structure.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Prepare an accurate mould and apply release agent.</li><li>Apply the gel coat/first surface layer evenly.</li><li>Build up resin and glass-fibre layers in the correct ratio.</li><li>Roll the laminate to spread resin and remove trapped air.</li><li>Add roving/reinforcement where extra support is needed.</li><li>Leave to cure fully.</li><li>Remove from the mould and trim accurately.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> panels, casings, boats and slides.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> creates strong, large and organic composite shapes.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> labour intensive; incorrect resin ratio or trapped air weakens the laminate.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> mould accuracy, release agent, even resin, correct resin/hardener ratio, air removal, cure time, reinforcement and trimming.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> strong ventilation/extraction, eye protection, resin contact and heat during curing.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Injection moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Molten thermoplastic is injected under pressure into a closed mould.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Feed dry granules into the hopper.</li><li>The Archimedes screw conveys and melts the polymer through heater bands.</li><li>Inject through the nozzle, sprue, runners and gates into the cavity.</li><li>Apply holding pressure to compensate for shrinkage.</li><li>Cool the mould until the part solidifies.</li><li>Open and eject using ejector pins.</li><li>Inspect and remove unwanted flash/sprue.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> casings, components and complex repeated shapes.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> fast, accurate and repeatable for high quantities; complex detail can be moulded.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> high mould/tooling cost; incorrect settings cause defects.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> dry material, heat, complete fill, holding pressure, shrinkage, flash, short shots, parting lines and dimensions.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot nozzle, molten polymer and heated machinery.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Blow moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A hot hollow tube (parison) is inflated inside a mould.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Extrude the parison and control wall thickness.</li><li>Close the mould around it.</li><li>Inject compressed air so it expands against the mould.</li><li>Cool in the mould.</li><li>Open and eject.</li><li>Trim flash and inspect for leaks and thickness variation.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> bottles and hollow containers.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> efficient production of seamless hollow forms.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> wall thickness can vary; flash needs trimming.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> wall thickness, sealing, blow pressure, heat, cooling, seams and leaks.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot polymer and ventilation.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Rotational moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Polymer powder melts and coats the inside of a heated mould rotating on two axes.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Measure and load polymer powder.</li><li>Heat while rotating the mould biaxially.</li><li>Allow the powder to melt and coat the inside.</li><li>Continue rotation during cooling.</li><li>Open and remove the hollow product.</li><li>Inspect for voids, bubbles and wall consistency.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> tanks and road dividers.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> large seamless hollow products.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> slow heating/cooling cycle; wall consistency requires good control.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> powder quantity, temperature, rotation, pooling, cooling and wall thickness.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot mould and rotating machinery.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Extrusion</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Molten thermoplastic is pushed continuously through a die to make a constant cross-section.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Feed granules into the hopper.</li><li>The Archimedes screw conveys and melts them through heated zones.</li><li>Force molten polymer through the shaped die.</li><li>Calibrate the profile with sizing plates/mandrels.</li><li>Cool with water or air.</li><li>Pull through with a haul-off unit and cut to length.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> pipes, hose pipes, window edging and continuous profiles.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> efficient continuous production.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> limited to constant cross-sections; poor cooling can distort the shape.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> material consistency, heat, die, dimensions, shrinkage, cooling and surface finish.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot die and molten polymer.</p></div></section>
