<section class="note-panel"><h2>Polymer Processes</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>These notes follow the terminology and detail in the teacher material, with each process kept to a concise but properly explained sequence.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Vacuum forming</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A heated thermoplastic sheet is shaped over a mould/former by removing the air underneath it.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Place the mould/former in the vacuum former and clamp the thermoplastic sheet securely in the frame above it.</li><li>Heat the sheet evenly with the radiant heater until the polymer softens and begins to sag uniformly.</li><li>Raise the mould into the softened sheet and switch on the vacuum pump so air is removed through the vent holes.</li><li>Atmospheric pressure pulls the sheet tightly over the mould; keep the vacuum applied while the polymer cools and sets to the required shape.</li><li>Release the vacuum, lower/remove the mould and take out the formed sheet.</li><li>Trim away the excess material and check the moulding for webbing, thinning, incomplete forming and dimensional accuracy.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> blister packaging, food trays, yoghurt pots and protective covers.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> relatively low-cost moulds; good for batch production; moulds are easy to modify.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> trimming creates waste; deep shapes can become thin; poor mould design can cause webbing or incomplete forming.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> even heating, tapered sides/draft angles, rounded corners, smooth mould surface and adequate air holes; check full mould contact and wall thickness.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> burns from heaters/hot plastic, moving platen and sharp trimmed edges.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Thermoforming / pressure forming</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A heated thermoplastic sheet is formed between matched positive and negative moulds using vacuum and pressure.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Clamp the thermoplastic sheet securely and heat it evenly until it reaches its forming temperature.</li><li>Position the softened sheet accurately between the matched positive and negative moulds.</li><li>Close the moulds so the sheet is held securely between them.</li><li>Apply vacuum and pressure so the plastic is forced accurately into the mould details and deeper features.</li><li>Keep the sheet held in the mould while it cools and solidifies.</li><li>Open the mould, remove the component, trim it and check the detail, thickness and dimensions.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> high-quality packaging, food containers and formed panels.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> deeper forms and sharper detail than simple vacuum forming; logos and textures reproduce clearly.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> matched tooling is more complex/costly; uneven heating can cause distortion or thickness variation.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> clarity of detail, depth, trimming accuracy, even heating and wall-thickness consistency.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> burns from heated sheet/moulds and crush risk from closing moulds.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Compression moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A measured charge of material is shaped inside a heated mould using heat and pressure.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Clean and preheat the mould, then accurately measure the required charge of moulding material.</li><li>Place the charge into the heated negative mould cavity.</li><li>Close the positive mould using the press so heat and pressure force the material to flow throughout the cavity.</li><li>Maintain the pressure while the material cures and takes the mould shape.</li><li>Open the mould and remove/eject the component once it has set sufficiently.</li><li>Trim any flash and inspect the component for complete filling, voids, surface defects and dimensional accuracy.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> electrical components and thermoset/rubber mouldings.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> repeatable process producing strong, dense components.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> curing takes time; excess flash may need trimming; charge mass must be controlled.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> charge weight, full cavity filling, pressure, cure, voids, flash and dimensions.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot moulds, high pressure, crushing and fumes.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Lamination / hand lay-up composites</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Resin and fibre reinforcement are built up in a mould to create a GRP/CFRP composite component.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Prepare the negative mould, clean it and apply a release agent so the finished component can be removed.</li><li>Apply the pigmented gel coat evenly to create the outer surface and allow it to become tacky.</li><li>Lay the fibre reinforcement into the mould and apply resin using the correct resin/catalyst ratio.</li><li>Use rollers to spread the resin through the fibres and remove trapped air, adding further layers or roving where extra reinforcement is required.</li><li>Leave the laminate to cure fully to the required thickness.</li><li>Remove the cured component from the mould, trim it accurately and check for bubbles, dry fibres, voids or delamination.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> boat hulls, car body panels, kayaks and large composite structures.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> relatively low setup cost; suitable for large and complex shapes.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> labour intensive and time-consuming; incorrect resin ratio or trapped air creates weak points.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> resin/catalyst ratio, even resin coverage, air removal, reinforcement, cure and laminate thickness.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> resin fumes and skin/eye irritation; glass/carbon-fibre dust during trimming; use ventilation/extraction and PPE.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Line bending</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A strip heater softens a narrow line in thermoplastic sheet so it can be bent accurately around a jig/former.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Cut the thermoplastic sheet to size, mark the bend line and position it directly above the strip heater.</li><li>Heat the marked line evenly until the polymer becomes soft; thicker material may need turning to heat both sides.</li><li>Remove the softened sheet and bend it around the prepared jig/former to the required angle.</li><li>Hold the sheet against the jig while it cools so it sets and does not spring back.</li><li>Remove it from the jig and inspect the angle, dimensions and surface for bubbling, burning or cracking.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> acrylic display stands, guards, brackets and signage.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> low-cost and accurate for straight bends.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> limited to local straight bends; overheating can damage the polymer.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> bend-line position, even heating, jig angle and absence of bubbles/burn marks.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> burns from the heating element and hot polymer.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Injection moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Molten thermoplastic is injected under high pressure into a closed metal mould.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Feed thermoplastic granules through the hopper into the heated barrel, where the rotating Archimedes screw moves them forward and the material melts.</li><li>As molten polymer collects in front of the screw, the screw winds back so a measured amount of material is prepared for the moulding cycle.</li><li>Close and clamp the mould halves, then move the screw forward like a ram to force the molten polymer through the nozzle and into the mould cavity.</li><li>Maintain pressure while the cavity fills, then allow the polymer to cool and solidify inside the mould.</li><li>Open the mould and use ejector pins to release the finished component.</li><li>Remove the sprue/flash where required and check for short shots, flash, sink marks, warping and incorrect dimensions.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> bottle caps, casings, toys, clips and detailed plastic components.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> very fast and repeatable for mass production; excellent detail and surface finish.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> high mould/tooling cost; incorrect machine settings can repeatedly produce defective parts.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> melt temperature, injection pressure, complete filling, shrinkage, flash, short shots and dimensions.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> high temperature and pressure, hot nozzle/molten polymer and crushing at the mould.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Extrusion</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Molten thermoplastic is forced continuously through a shaped die to produce a constant cross-section.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Feed plastic granules through the hopper into the heated barrel.</li><li>A motor turns the Archimedes screw, moving the granules forward while heat and friction melt the polymer.</li><li>The screw forces the molten polymer continuously through a shaped die which forms the required cross-section.</li><li>The hot extrusion passes through a cooling stage using air or water so it solidifies without excessive distortion.</li><li>The finished extrusion is pulled through continuously and cut to the required length or wound onto reels.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> solid rods, hose pipes, pipes, window profiles and other continuous sections.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> efficient continuous production and low unit cost at high volumes.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> limited to constant cross-sections; poor cooling can cause distortion.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> barrel/die temperature, material flow, cross-section dimensions and cooling.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot die/molten polymer, moving machinery and cutting hazards.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Blow moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> A heated hollow tube called a parison is inflated inside a two-part mould to produce a hollow plastic product.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Form the heated thermoplastic parison and position it between the open halves of the mould.</li><li>Close and clamp the mould around the parison so it is sealed in position.</li><li>Force compressed air into the parison so it expands and presses against the inside surfaces of the mould.</li><li>Keep the product in the mould while the polymer cools and solidifies to the required hollow shape.</li><li>Open the mould and remove the component.</li><li>Trim excess material and inspect the walls, neck/seams and product for leaks.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> shampoo and detergent bottles and other hollow containers.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> efficient mass production of seamless hollow forms.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> wall thickness can vary and excess material may require trimming.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> wall/base thickness, blow pressure, sealing, neck accuracy and leak testing.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot polymer/moulds, compressed air and moving mould parts.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Rotational moulding</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Polymer powder melts and coats the inside of a heated mould while the mould rotates on two axes.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Measure the required quantity of polymer powder/granules, place it inside the mould and seal the mould.</li><li>Heat the mould while it rotates slowly on two axes so the polymer begins to melt.</li><li>Continue rotating as the molten polymer repeatedly coats the inside surfaces and builds the hollow wall.</li><li>Move the mould to the cooling stage but continue rotation so the material does not pool or sag while solidifying.</li><li>Once cooled, stop the rotation, open the mould and remove the seamless hollow component.</li><li>Trim and inspect wall-thickness uniformity, incomplete fusion, porosity and dimensional accuracy.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> water tanks, wheelie bins, kayaks, traffic products and playground equipment.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> large seamless hollow forms and relatively lower tooling cost than injection moulding.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> long heating/cooling cycle and limited suitable materials.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> powder quantity, heating, biaxial rotation, cooling and wall thickness.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> hot moulds/ovens, rotating machinery and manual-handling risks.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Calendaring</h2><div class="study-card"><p><strong>What it is:</strong> Softened polymer is passed through a series of rollers to produce continuous sheet or film of controlled thickness.</p><h3>Step by step</h3><ol><li>Heat the polymer until it becomes soft and workable, then feed it into the calendaring rollers.</li><li>Pass the material through a series of heated rollers with progressively smaller gaps so its thickness is reduced evenly.</li><li>Where required, pass the sheet through an embossing roller to add a texture or pattern.</li><li>Pass the formed sheet through chill rollers so it cools while maintaining the required thickness and surface.</li><li>Trim the sheet edges and wind the finished material onto rolls or cut it into sheets.</li></ol><p><strong>Common uses:</strong> PVC sheets, vinyl flooring and plastic films.</p><p><strong>Advantages:</strong> smooth, uniform sheet with good thickness control; efficient continuous production.</p><p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> high setup cost and large equipment; not economical for short runs.</p><p><strong>Quality control:</strong> sheet thickness, surface/embossing quality, roller temperature, gap and alignment.</p><p><strong>Safety:</strong> crushing/entanglement at rollers, burns, fumes and need for guards/emergency stops.</p></div></section>
