## Jigs and fixtures

Jigs and fixtures are manufacturing aids used to improve **accuracy, consistency and repeatability**. They are particularly useful when the same operation must be repeated because they reduce the need to measure and mark out every component from scratch.

### Jigs

A **jig holds or locates the workpiece and also guides the tool**. A drilling jig, for example, can use guide holes or bushes so each hole is drilled in the same position and at the same angle.

Using a jig can:

- reduce repeated measuring and marking out;
- speed up manufacture;
- reduce human error;
- improve consistency between components;
- help produce interchangeable parts;
- allow an operation to be repeated accurately by different operators.

### Fixtures

A **fixture holds and locates the workpiece securely but does not guide the cutting tool**. It keeps the component in the correct position while an operation such as machining, welding, assembly or inspection takes place.

Both jigs and fixtures rely on consistent locating points so the workpiece is positioned in the same place each time.

### Design considerations

A useful jig or fixture must suit the manufacturing operation as well as the required dimensions.

- **Accuracy:** locating surfaces, stops and guide points must correspond to the required dimensions and tolerances.
- **Rigidity:** the device must resist movement under manufacturing forces.
- **Speed:** components should be quick to load, locate, clamp and remove.
- **Safety:** the workpiece must be held securely and the design should help keep the operator away from dangerous moving tools.
- **Cost:** making a jig or fixture creates an initial cost, so it becomes more worthwhile as the number of repeated components increases.
- **Quality:** consistent positioning reduces variation, rework and rejected components.

### Production scale

For a one-off component, making a dedicated jig may take longer than careful measuring and marking out. In **batch production**, however, a jig or fixture can save time on every repeated component while improving consistency. In **mass production**, dedicated tooling, fixtures and automated positioning can be integrated into the production system to maintain repeatability at high speed.

The main benefit is therefore not simply faster production: fixed locating and guiding features reduce repeated manual measurement and variation, producing more consistent components with fewer errors.
