<section class="note-panel"><h2>Jigs and Fixtures</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Jigs and fixtures are manufacturing aids used to improve <strong>accuracy, consistency and repeatability</strong>. They are especially useful in batch and mass production because the same operation can be repeated without marking out every component from scratch.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Jig</h3><p>A <strong>jig holds or locates the work and also guides the tool</strong>. For example, a drilling jig can use bushes or guide holes so that every hole is drilled in the same position and at the same angle.</p><ul><li>Reduces repeated measuring and marking out.</li><li>Speeds up manufacture.</li><li>Reduces human error.</li><li>Produces interchangeable components more consistently.</li><li>Can allow less-skilled operators to repeat an operation accurately.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Fixture</h3><p>A <strong>fixture holds and locates the work securely but does not guide the cutting tool</strong>. It keeps a component in the correct position while an operation such as machining, welding, assembly or inspection is carried out.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Jig vs fixture</h3><p><strong>Remember:</strong> both locate the work, but a <strong>jig guides the tool</strong>; a <strong>fixture only holds/locates the work</strong>.</p><p>Good designs use datum faces, stops, clamps and locating features so components are positioned in exactly the same place each time. The jig or fixture must also be rigid enough not to move under manufacturing forces.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Design and manufacturing considerations</h3><ul><li><strong>Accuracy:</strong> locating surfaces and guide points must match the required dimensions and tolerances.</li><li><strong>Speed:</strong> components should be quick to load, locate, clamp and remove.</li><li><strong>Safety:</strong> the work must be held securely and hands kept away from dangerous operations.</li><li><strong>Cost:</strong> designing and making a jig has an initial cost, so it is most worthwhile when enough identical components are being produced.</li><li><strong>Quality:</strong> consistent positioning reduces variation and rejected parts.</li></ul></div><div class="exam-tip"><strong>Exam link:</strong> do not just say that a jig makes production faster. Explain the chain: fixed locating points/guide → less repeated marking out and human variation → faster, more accurate repeated manufacture → fewer rejects and lower unit cost.</div></section>
