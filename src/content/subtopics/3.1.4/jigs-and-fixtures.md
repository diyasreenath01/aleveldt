<section class="note-panel"><h2>Jigs and Fixtures</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Jigs and fixtures improve <strong>accuracy, repeatability and production speed</strong>, especially when the same operation is carried out repeatedly.</p></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Key difference</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Jig</h3><p>Holds or locates the workpiece and <strong>guides the tool</strong>.</p><ul><li><strong>Example:</strong> a drilling jig positions the component and guides the drill through bushes.</li><li><strong>Benefit:</strong> reduces repeated marking out and keeps holes in the same position.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Fixture</h3><p>Holds or locates the workpiece securely but <strong>does not guide the tool</strong>.</p><ul><li><strong>Example:</strong> a fixture holds a component in a fixed orientation during machining or assembly.</li><li><strong>Benefit:</strong> improves consistency and reduces movement or setup error.</li></ul></div></div></section>
<section class="note-panel"><h2>Why they are used</h2><div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Accuracy and repeatability</h3><ul><li>Components are positioned from the same datum each time.</li><li>Dimensions and angles can be controlled consistently.</li><li>Human marking-out error is reduced.</li></ul></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Production efficiency</h3><ul><li>Setup and marking-out time can be reduced.</li><li>They are particularly useful in batch and mass production.</li><li>The cost of making a dedicated jig or fixture must be justified by the quantity and accuracy required.</li></ul></div></div></section>
