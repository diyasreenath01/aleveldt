## Iterative design process

An **iterative design process** develops a solution through repeated cycles of investigation, designing, modelling, testing, evaluation and refinement. There is no single fixed route through a design challenge: evidence gathered during development can cause the designer to return to an earlier decision and improve it.

### Designing around people

A good design process begins by identifying **needs, wants and values** rather than deciding on a solution immediately. Designers can gather primary and secondary data through **market research, interviews, human factors research, focus groups, product analysis and evaluation**.

**Anthropometric data and percentiles** help designers choose dimensions appropriate for the intended population. **Ergonomic data** helps designers consider comfort, safety, usability and interaction.

### Developing a proposal

Research should directly influence the design proposal. Designers generate and develop ideas, then plan and manufacture prototype solutions. Sketches, CAD, models and prototypes allow ideas to be communicated and tested before final manufacture.

### Testing, evaluating and refining

Evaluation takes place throughout development rather than only at the end. Designers test prototypes, obtain feedback, identify weaknesses and use the evidence to inform the next version.

The cycle can be represented as:

**Investigate → Design → Model/Prototype → Test → Evaluate → Refine → Test again**

The process continues until the proposal satisfies the relevant user needs and design requirements as effectively as possible.
