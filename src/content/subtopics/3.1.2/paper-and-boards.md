## Paper and boards

Paper and board are available in different **weights, thicknesses, surface finishes and structures**. Selection depends on the product's function, printing requirements, strength, appearance, cost and disposal.

### Important characteristics

- **grammage (gsm):** mass per square metre; higher gsm generally means a heavier sheet
- **thickness/stiffness:** important for packaging structure and resistance to bending
- **surface finish:** affects appearance, print quality and suitability for coatings
- **absorbency:** affects inks, adhesives and moisture resistance
- **folding/creasing behaviour:** important for cartons and packaging nets
- **printability:** important for graphics, branding and information.

### Typical choices

**Cartridge paper** is suitable for drawing and general printing. **Tracing paper** is translucent and useful for overlays. **Layout paper** is thin and useful for rapid design development. **Corrugated board** combines liners with a fluted core to give stiffness and cushioning with relatively low mass, making it useful for transport packaging. **Solid board** can provide a smoother, denser surface for cartons and presentation packaging.

### Design considerations

Paper-based materials can often be cut, creased, folded, printed and recycled efficiently. However, untreated paper and board are vulnerable to water and may need coatings or laminates, which can make recycling more difficult.

### Exam approach

Avoid choosing 'cardboard' without explanation. State the specific form and link it to the application:

**corrugated board → fluted structure gives stiffness and impact cushioning at low mass → protects products during transport without excessive packaging weight**.
