## Modern materials

A **modern material** is a material developed or adapted to provide properties or manufacturing possibilities that improve on more traditional choices for particular applications.

AQA expects you to be familiar with **Kevlar, precious metal clay (PMC), high-density modelling foam and Polymorph**. The important skill is explaining why a material suits an application.

### Kevlar

Kevlar is an aramid fibre with a very high strength-to-weight ratio and good resistance to impact and abrasion. It is used in applications such as protective equipment, reinforcement and high-performance products where low mass and strength matter.

**Talking point:** strong and lightweight can improve protection or performance without the mass of a thicker metal component, although specialist fibres can be more expensive and difficult to process.

### Precious metal clay (PMC)

PMC contains fine precious-metal particles mixed with a binder. It can be shaped in a clay-like state and then fired; the binder burns away and the metal particles sinter together.

**Useful for:** intricate jewellery and small decorative products because complex forms can be created before firing.

### High-density modelling foam

High-density modelling foam can be cut, shaped and machined easily to create accurate models and prototypes. It is useful for testing form, ergonomics and appearance without manufacturing the final product in an expensive material.

### Polymorph

Polymorph is a low-temperature thermoplastic that softens when heated and can be hand formed, then becomes rigid again on cooling. It can be reheated and reshaped.

**Useful for:** quick models, ergonomic trials, custom grips and prototype components.

### Exam approach

Use **property → application → benefit**. Naming the material alone gains little; explain what its properties allow the designer or product to do.
