## Modern materials

A **modern material** is a material developed or adapted to provide properties or manufacturing possibilities that improve on more traditional choices for particular applications.

AQA expects you to be familiar with **Kevlar, precious metal clay (PMC), high-density modelling foam and Polymorph**. The important point is the connection between a material's properties and the new design possibilities those properties create.

### Kevlar

Kevlar is an aramid fibre with a very high strength-to-weight ratio and good resistance to impact and abrasion. Its performance depends on how the fibres are arranged and used. **Woven Kevlar fabric** can provide flexible, lightweight protection, while **Kevlar used as reinforcement in a composite** can produce a stiffer structural component. Fibre direction matters because reinforcement is strongest when fibres are aligned with the main tensile loads.

**Design possibilities:** high tensile strength at low mass allows designers to reduce weight while retaining protection or reinforcement. This makes Kevlar useful in protective clothing, cut-resistant products, tyres and high-performance composite components. However, it is more difficult to cut and process than many conventional fibres and is difficult to recycle when bonded into a composite.

### Precious metal clay (PMC)

PMC contains fine precious-metal particles mixed with a binder. It can be shaped in a clay-like state and then fired; the binder burns away and the metal particles sinter together.

**Design possibilities:** because the material is soft and mouldable before firing, intricate textures and small complex forms can be produced without traditional metal-forming skills or equipment. This makes it useful for detailed jewellery and decorative products. Shrinkage during firing must be allowed for when dimensions are important.

### High-density modelling foam

High-density modelling foam can be cut, shaped and machined easily to create accurate models and prototypes.

**Design possibilities:** its low cost and ease of machining allow designers to make full-size or scale models to investigate form, ergonomics, proportions and appearance before committing to expensive final materials or tooling. It is primarily a modelling material rather than a substitute for testing the final material's structural performance.

### Polymorph

Polymorph is a low-temperature thermoplastic that softens when heated and can be hand formed, then becomes rigid again on cooling. It can be reheated and reshaped.

**Design possibilities:** repeated heating and reshaping makes rapid physical iteration possible, so designers can test custom grips, ergonomic forms, fittings and prototype components directly against a user or product and then modify them quickly.

### Material choice and sustainability

A material being lightweight, modern or high-performance does **not automatically make the product more sustainable**. Lower mass can reduce material use and transport or operating energy, but this must be balanced against raw-material extraction, energy-intensive manufacture, product lifespan, repairability and end-of-life recovery. For example, a lightweight fibre-reinforced composite may reduce energy use during service but be harder to separate and recycle than a heavier single-material component.
