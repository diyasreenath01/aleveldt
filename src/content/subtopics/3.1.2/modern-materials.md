## Modern materials

A **modern material** is developed or adapted to provide properties or manufacturing possibilities that improve on traditional choices for a particular application. Modern materials can help designers solve **design problems, technical constraints and environmental issues**.

AQA expects familiarity with **Kevlar, precious metal clay (PMC), high-density modelling foam and Polymorph**.

### Kevlar

Kevlar is an aramid fibre with a **very high strength-to-weight ratio**, good impact and abrasion resistance, resistance to stretching and useful stability at high temperatures.

Its performance depends on how the fibres are used. **Woven Kevlar fabric** can provide flexible lightweight protection, while Kevlar used as reinforcement in a composite can produce a stiffer structural component. Fibre direction matters because reinforcement performs best when the fibres are arranged to carry the main tensile loads.

**Applications:** protective clothing, cut-resistant products, tyres, high-performance sports products, boats and composite components. Its low mass is particularly valuable in transport and performance products where reducing weight can improve efficiency or handling.

Kevlar is harder to cut and process than many conventional fibres, and recycling becomes difficult when it is permanently bonded into a composite. High performance therefore needs to justify the additional manufacturing and end-of-life difficulty.

### Precious metal clay (PMC)

PMC contains fine precious-metal particles mixed with a binder. It can be shaped in a clay-like state and then fired; the binder burns away and the metal particles sinter together.

Because it is soft and mouldable before firing, designers can create intricate textures and small complex forms without relying entirely on traditional metal-forming methods. It is particularly useful for jewellery and decorative products. **Shrinkage during firing** must be allowed for where dimensions are important.

### High-density modelling foam

High-density modelling foam can be **cut, shaped and machined easily** to produce accurate models and prototypes.

It allows designers to investigate form, ergonomics, proportions and appearance before committing to expensive final materials or tooling. It is therefore valuable for iterative development, but a foam model cannot automatically prove the structural performance of the intended production material.

### Polymorph

Polymorph is a **low-temperature thermoplastic** that softens when heated, can be hand formed and becomes rigid again on cooling. It can be reheated and reshaped repeatedly.

This makes it useful for rapid physical iteration, custom grips, ergonomic forms, fittings and prototype components. A designer can test a form against a user, reheat it and alter it quickly rather than manufacturing a completely new prototype each time.

### Choosing a modern material

A modern material should be selected because its particular characteristics solve the design requirement, not simply because it is new. High performance may bring trade-offs in **cost, processing, repair and recycling**. A lightweight material can reduce material use or energy during transport and service, but its complete life cycle still needs to be considered.
