# Performance characteristics of polymers

Polymers are widely used because their properties can be tailored to the product and manufacturing process. AQA expects you to know the difference between **thermoplastics and thermosetting polymers**, the named polymers below, their relevant properties and why they suit particular applications.

## Polymer classifications

### Thermoplastics

**Thermoplastics soften when heated and harden when cooled.** This can be repeated, so they can usually be reshaped and are generally easier to recycle than thermosets. Their polymer chains are not permanently cross-linked.

### Thermosetting polymers

**Thermosets undergo an irreversible chemical change when cured.** Strong cross-links form between polymer chains, so they cannot simply be reheated and remoulded. They tend to have good heat resistance and dimensional stability.

## Thermoplastics you must know

| Polymer | Key characteristics | Typical applications / why |
|---|---|---|
| **LDPE – low density polyethylene** | Flexible, tough, lightweight, chemical and moisture resistant; good electrical insulator | Carrier bags, flexible packaging, film, squeeze bottles and wire insulation |
| **HDPE – high density polyethylene** | Stiffer and stronger than LDPE, tough, chemical/water resistant | Bottles, containers, buckets, pipes, toys |
| **PP – polypropylene** | Tough, lightweight, chemical resistant, fatigue resistant; can form a **living hinge** | Hinged boxes, food containers, folders, automotive components |
| **HIPS – high impact polystyrene** | Tough, impact resistant, inexpensive and easily thermoformed | Vacuum-formed packaging, refrigerator liners, model making |
| **ABS – acrylonitrile butadiene styrene** | Tough, rigid, impact resistant, good surface finish | Protective housings, toys, luggage, automotive trim |
| **PMMA – polymethyl methacrylate / acrylic** | Rigid, transparent, weather/UV resistant, good optical clarity; can be brittle | Signs, displays, light covers, windows and transparent guards |
| **Nylon (polyamide)** | Tough, hardwearing, low friction, good tensile strength | Gears, bearings, cable ties, textiles and mechanical components |
| **Rigid PVC** | Rigid, durable, chemical/weather resistant, good electrical insulation | Pipes, window frames, guttering, electrical trunking |
| **Flexible PVC** | PVC with plasticiser; flexible, durable and waterproof | Cable insulation, flexible tubing, flooring and synthetic leather |
| **PET – polyethylene terephthalate** | Tough, lightweight, transparent, good barrier properties, widely recyclable | Drinks bottles, food packaging and polyester fibres |

## Thermosets you must know

| Polymer | Key characteristics | Typical applications / why |
|---|---|---|
| **UF – urea formaldehyde** | Hard, rigid, heat resistant and good electrical insulator; relatively brittle | Electrical fittings and as a resin/binder in manufactured boards |
| **MF – melamine formaldehyde** | Hard, scratch and heat resistant, hygienic surface | Laminated worktops, tableware and surface laminates |
| **Polyester resin** | Rigid thermosetting resin, can be cast and used as a composite matrix | GRP boat hulls, car panels, cast resin products |
| **Epoxy resin** | Strong adhesive, good chemical resistance, low shrinkage, excellent bonding | Structural adhesives, coatings and CFRP/GRP matrix resin |

## Performance characteristics AQA can ask about

You should be able to connect the named polymer to the following characteristics rather than memorising a material with no application:

- **toughness** and resistance to impact
- **elasticity/flexibility**
- **thermal and electrical insulation**
- **UV resistance**
- ability to be **moulded**
- resistance to **chemicals and liquids**
- melting/softening behaviour
- suitability for **food packaging**
- **biodegradability** and **recyclability**
- ability to be **self-finishing**
- ability to be combined with **other polymers or additives**.

Polymers are often self-finishing because colour and surface texture can be created during moulding, reducing secondary finishing operations. Additives can further change behaviour: plasticisers increase flexibility, pigments add colour, flame retardants reduce combustion, UV stabilisers slow UV degradation and fillers can reduce cost or alter processing/performance. ## Stock forms

AQA expects recognition of common polymer stock forms: **sheet, film, granules, rod/other extruded sections, foam and powder**.

Polymer film is generally thin and flexible, whereas polymer sheet is thicker and available in different stock thicknesses. ## Polymer-based sheet and film examples

| Material | Important characteristic | Typical application |
|---|---|---|
| **Foam board** | Lightweight, rigid layered sheet; easy to cut | Presentation and architectural models |
| **Fluted PP** | Tough, lightweight, foldable, impact resistant and rigid | Signs, boxes and reusable packaging |
| **Translucent PP sheet** | Tough, fatigue resistant and foldable | Packaging and folders |
| **Styrofoam** | Lightweight closed-cell foam, easily cut/shaped | Models, formers and packaging |
| **LDPE sheet/film** | Thin, flexible, chemical resistant, moisture barrier | Wrapping, bags and packaging |
| **Plastazote** | Tough, flexible closed-cell foam with good impact resistance | Protective packaging, mats and flotation products |
| **Cellulose acetate** | Tough, transparent/translucent and biodegradable | Transparent film and packaging |
| **PLA sheet/film** | Bio-based/biodegradable under appropriate conditions | Short-life biodegradable packaging |

## Exam focus

Always connect **property → product requirement → material choice**. For example: **PP is suitable for a hinged food container because it has excellent fatigue resistance, allowing a thin moulded section to flex repeatedly as a living hinge without quickly breaking.**
