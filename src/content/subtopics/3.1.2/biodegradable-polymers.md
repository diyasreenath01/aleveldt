# Biodegradable polymers

Biodegradable polymers are polymers designed to **break down after use** through environmental or biological action. AQA requires you to understand their suitability for products, how they degrade, and the named examples below.

## Biopolymer vs biodegradable polymer

These terms do not mean exactly the same thing.

- A **biopolymer** is produced from biological/renewable sources or by living organisms.
- A **biodegradable polymer** is defined by its ability to break down after use into simpler products through biological/environmental action.
- A polymer can therefore be bio-based without necessarily being readily biodegradable, while some biodegradable polymers can be manufactured synthetically.

Many bio-based polymers use renewable raw materials such as **starch, cellulose, vegetable oils and sugars** rather than relying entirely on finite crude-oil resources. fileciteturn39file2L23-L41

## How biodegradable polymers degrade

Biodegradable polymers can be designed to degrade through the action of **UV light, water or enzymes/microorganisms in soil**. The structure of the polymer affects how easily it breaks down. Under suitable conditions microorganisms can convert biodegradable material into products such as **water, carbon dioxide and biomass**; methane can also be produced under anaerobic conditions. fileciteturn39file2L83-L91

The conditions matter. Some materials marketed as compostable need controlled **industrial composting conditions**, so biodegradable does not mean a product will immediately disappear if it is littered or placed in an ordinary home compost heap.

## AQA named biodegradable polymers

| Polymer | Source / type | Important characteristics | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corn-starch polymer** | Starch from crops such as corn/maize | Renewable, biodegradable/compostable, food-safe and resistant to fats and oils | Packaging, bags, cups, disposable cutlery and takeaway containers |
| **Potatopak** | Starch-based material, particularly potato starch | Biodegradable and suited to short-life disposable products | Disposable food-service and packaging products |
| **Biopol / bio-batch systems** | Biodegradable polymer/additive systems | Designed to reduce persistence compared with conventional polymers | Short-life products and packaging |
| **Polyactide / polylactic acid (PLA)** | Produced from fermented plant sugars/starches such as maize, corn or sugar cane | Good strength and low thermal expansion but relatively poor heat resistance; can be moulded or made into film | Food packaging, disposable cutlery and some medical applications |
| **Polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA)** | Biopolymer produced by microorganisms | Biodegradable; can be processed into films and moulded products | Packaging, disposable products and some medical applications |
| **Lactide / glycolide based polymers** | Biodegradable synthetic polymers | Can break down in the body; useful where a temporary component is required | Medical implants, sutures, pins and other temporary medical devices |
| **Water-soluble biodegradable film (e.g. ecofilm)** | Specially formulated biodegradable/water-soluble polymer | Dissolves or breaks down when exposed to water under appropriate conditions | Soluble bags and specialist packaging |

## Advantages

- Can reduce dependence on **finite fossil resources** when renewable feedstocks are used.
- Useful for **short-life products** where long-term durability after disposal is unnecessary.
- Can be processed into **film or 3D moulded products**.
- Some are compostable under the correct conditions.
- Certain biodegradable polymers are useful medically because they can safely break down after completing their function.

## Limitations and environmental issues

Biodegradable does **not automatically mean environmentally harmless**.

- Growing crops for polymer feedstock requires land, water and energy and can compete with food production or contribute to land-use change.
- Decomposition in anaerobic conditions can produce **methane**, a greenhouse gas.
- Some materials require specific heat, moisture or industrial composting conditions before they degrade effectively.
- If incompatible biodegradable plastics enter conventional plastic recycling streams they can contaminate the recycled material.
- Additives and the complete life cycle of the product still need to be considered. fileciteturn39file2L48-L66

## Exam focus

For an AQA question, do not simply write **“it is environmentally friendly because it biodegrades.”** Link the named material to the product and explain the relevant property. For example: **PLA is suitable for disposable food packaging because it can be formed into sheet/film and can biodegrade under suitable composting conditions, reducing the need for a permanently durable polymer in a short-life product.**
