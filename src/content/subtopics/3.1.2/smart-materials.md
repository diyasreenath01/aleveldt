<section class="note-panel"><h2>Smart Materials</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>A <strong>smart material</strong> changes one or more of its properties in a useful and predictable way when it responds to an external stimulus such as <strong>temperature, light or pressure/force</strong>. The important exam skill is to connect the stimulus → response → useful product function.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Shape memory alloys (SMA) — e.g. Nitinol</h3><p>Can be deformed and then return towards a previously set shape when heated. This can replace a conventional actuator or mechanism in some products.</p><p><strong>Applications:</strong> spectacle frames, orthodontic wires, medical stents and temperature-activated mechanisms.</p><p><strong>Talking point:</strong> compact and can simplify a mechanism, but temperature control, response speed and cost must suit the application.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Thermochromic pigments</h3><p>Change colour as temperature changes.</p><p><strong>Applications:</strong> temperature indicators, baby-feeding products, mugs, packaging and warning labels.</p><p><strong>Design value:</strong> gives the user visual feedback without a separate electronic display.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Photochromic pigments</h3><p>Change colour or darken in response to light, particularly UV.</p><p><strong>Application:</strong> photochromic spectacle lenses darken in bright conditions, reducing glare while also helping protect the eyes from UV radiation.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Phosphorescent pigments</h3><p>Absorb light energy and release it slowly afterwards, producing an afterglow.</p><p><strong>Applications:</strong> emergency signs, watch faces, switches and wayfinding markings where visibility in darkness is useful.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Electroluminescent wire</h3><p>Emits light when an electrical current/field is applied. It is thin and flexible, so it can create illuminated outlines and decorative or safety features around curved forms.</p><p><strong>Applications:</strong> clothing, signage, vehicle interiors and product detailing.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Piezoelectric materials</h3><p>Can produce an electrical charge when mechanically stressed; the effect can also be used in reverse to create small movements or vibrations.</p><p><strong>Applications:</strong> pressure/impact sensors, buzzers, electronic ignition and vibration sensing.</p><p><strong>Talking point:</strong> they can convert mechanical and electrical energy without a conventional moving mechanism.</p></div><div class="exam-tip"><strong>Exam technique:</strong> avoid simply naming a smart material. Write the full chain: <em>stimulus → material response → product benefit</em>. Example: UV light → photochromic lens darkens → glare is reduced for the user.</div></section>
