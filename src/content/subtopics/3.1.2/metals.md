## Metals

Metals are selected by linking their properties to the product and manufacturing process. Important characteristics include **hardness, toughness, malleability, ductility, tensile and compressive strength, density, corrosion and wear resistance, thermal/electrical conductivity, melting point, machinability, castability and weldability**.

### Ferrous, non-ferrous and alloys

**Ferrous metals** are iron-based. Examples include low-carbon steel, medium-carbon steel, stainless steel, high-speed steel and cast iron.

**Non-ferrous metals** include aluminium, copper, zinc, titanium, tin, silver and gold. They are often selected where properties such as low density, conductivity or corrosion resistance are important.

An **alloy** combines a metal with one or more other elements to alter its properties. Examples include stainless steel, brass, bronze, duralumin and pewter.

### Important material choices

| Metal | Useful characteristics | Why / where it is suitable |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-carbon steel** | Under about 0.3% carbon; malleable, ductile, readily welded and relatively inexpensive | Fabricated products, sheet-metal work and structures where easy forming and joining are important |
| **Cast iron** | Good castability, compressive strength, wear resistance, heat retention and vibration damping | Engine blocks, machine bases, cookware and cast components; its damping is valuable around vibration |
| **Stainless steel** | Corrosion resistant, hardwearing and hygienic | Kitchen, food-processing and medical products where corrosion resistance and cleaning are important |
| **Aluminium** | Low density, corrosion resistant, malleable and a good thermal conductor | Transport, housings and products where reducing mass is important |
| **Copper** | Excellent electrical and thermal conductivity, ductile, malleable and corrosion resistant | Electrical wiring, electronics, plumbing and heat-transfer applications |
| **Brass** | Corrosion resistant, machinable and decorative | Fittings, valves, handles and decorative components |
| **Titanium** | High strength-to-weight ratio and excellent corrosion resistance | High-performance products where low mass and durability justify higher cost |

Copper's combination of conductivity and ductility is especially useful in wiring because it carries current efficiently while being drawn into thin wire. Its corrosion resistance and ability to form tube also make it useful for plumbing.

Cast iron shows why the word **strong** on its own is not enough. Its compressive strength, castability, wear resistance and vibration damping make it useful for heavy machine components, while these properties do not mean it is the best metal for every type of loading.

### Stock forms

Metals are commonly supplied as:

- **sheet:** relatively thin flat stock
- **plate:** thicker flat stock
- **bar:** solid sections such as flat, square or round bar
- **tube:** hollow round, square or other sections
- **structural sections:** profiles such as I/H sections, angles, channels and tee sections.

The stock form contributes to performance. A hollow tube can provide useful stiffness with less material than a solid section, while an I-section concentrates material where it contributes effectively to bending resistance. Selecting stock close to the final form can also reduce machining, waste and cost.

### Material selection

The most suitable metal depends on the actual requirement. Low density may matter more than maximum strength in a portable product; conductivity may dominate in an electrical component; corrosion resistance may be essential outdoors. A justified choice therefore names the **relevant property and explains its effect in the product**, rather than describing a metal only as “strong” or “durable”.
