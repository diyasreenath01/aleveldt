## Metals

Metals are selected by linking their properties to the product and manufacturing process. Important characteristics include **hardness, toughness, malleability, elasticity, tensile strength, density, corrosion resistance, thermal/electrical conductivity, melting point and ability to be joined or finished**.

### Ferrous and non-ferrous metals

**Ferrous metals** are iron-based. Examples include low-carbon steel, medium-carbon steel, stainless steel, high-speed steel and cast iron.

**Non-ferrous metals** include aluminium, copper, zinc, titanium, tin, silver and gold. They are often selected where properties such as low density, conductivity or corrosion resistance are important.

### Alloys

An **alloy** combines a metal with other elements to achieve improved or altered properties. Examples include stainless steel, brass, bronze, duralumin and pewter.

Useful examples:
- **low-carbon steel:** tough, formable, weldable and relatively inexpensive; widely used for fabricated products
- **stainless steel:** corrosion resistant and hygienic; useful for kitchen and medical applications
- **aluminium:** low density, corrosion resistant and easily formed; useful where mass matters
- **copper:** excellent electrical and thermal conductor; used in wiring and heat-transfer applications
- **brass:** corrosion resistant, machinable and decorative; useful for fittings
- **titanium:** high strength-to-weight ratio and corrosion resistance, but expensive; useful in demanding high-performance applications.

### Stock forms

Metals are supplied as **sheet, plate, bar, tube and structural sections** such as I-beams, channels and angles. Choosing an appropriate stock form can reduce machining, waste, time and cost.

### Exam approach

Do not say a metal is simply 'strong'. Name the relevant property. For example:

**aluminium → low density + corrosion resistance → lighter outdoor product requiring less protective finishing**.
