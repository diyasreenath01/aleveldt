# Composites

A **composite** combines two or more different materials so the resulting material gains useful properties that the individual materials do not provide as effectively on their own. In fibre composites, the **reinforcement** commonly provides tensile strength/stiffness while the **matrix** binds the reinforcement, transfers loads, protects it and helps provide compressive strength and shape. Your source uses MDF as a simple matrix/reinforcement analogy. fileciteturn39file1L3-L13

AQA expects you to explain **why a particular composite is suitable for an application**, including its enhanced physical/mechanical properties, ability to form 3D shapes, manufacture and product performance.

## AQA composites to know

| Composite | What it is made from | Key properties / why it is useful | Typical applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CFRP – carbon fibre reinforced plastic** | Woven carbon fibres in a polymer resin matrix, commonly epoxy | Exceptional **strength-to-weight ratio**, stiff, high tensile strength, corrosion/chemical resistance, low thermal expansion; expensive and difficult to repair/recycle | Aircraft structures, racing cars, bicycles, golf clubs, tennis rackets, protective equipment |
| **GRP – glass reinforced plastic** | Glass fibres/mat in polyester or epoxy resin | Good tensile and compressive performance, lightweight, corrosion resistant, can be moulded into complex 3D forms, durable; difficult to recycle/repair | Boat hulls, car body panels, tanks, roofing and large moulded shells |
| **Tungsten carbide** | Tungsten carbide ceramic particles bonded in a metallic binder such as cobalt – a **cermet** | Extremely hard, wear and scratch resistant, retains cutting ability; relatively brittle compared with tough metals | Cutting-tool tips, drill bits and wear-resistant tooling |
| **Aluminium composite board (ACB/ACP)** | Thin aluminium skins bonded to a polymer core | Lightweight, rigid, tough, corrosion resistant and good at damping vibration; smooth printable surface | Signs, cladding, hoardings, display panels and transport applications |
| **Concrete** | Cement, sand and aggregate mixed with water | Excellent **compressive strength** but poor tensile strength | Foundations, paths, blocks and structural elements mainly loaded in compression |
| **Reinforced concrete** | Concrete with steel reinforcement bars/mesh | Concrete carries compression while steel provides **tensile strength**, allowing larger structural spans | Bridges, high-rise structures, beams, floors and foundations |
| **Fibre cement** | Cement matrix reinforced with fibres, commonly cellulose | Tougher and less brittle than unreinforced cement, durable and weather resistant | Roofing, façade/cladding panels and building products |
| **Glulam – glued laminated timber** | Timber laminations bonded with durable adhesive with grain mainly parallel | High structural strength, stable, allows long/curved sections, attractive natural appearance and good strength-to-weight performance | Beams, arches, bridges, roofs, domes and large-span structures |

## CFRP

Carbon fibres are extremely fine and can be grouped into threads and woven into fabric before being bonded with a resin matrix. fileciteturn39file1L25-L28 The fibres provide very high tensile strength while the resin holds them in position and helps resist compression. This gives CFRP its outstanding strength-to-weight ratio. Your notes also highlight high chemical resistance, temperature tolerance and low thermal expansion. fileciteturn40file5L513-L539

The low mass is particularly important in aircraft and performance products: reducing aircraft mass reduces the energy/fuel required in use, while sports equipment can be made stiff and strong without becoming heavy. fileciteturn39file1L43-L46

## GRP

GRP combines glass fibre reinforcement with a thermosetting resin such as polyester or epoxy. The glass improves tensile performance while the resin provides the matrix and compressive support. fileciteturn40file5L548-L576

It is particularly useful for boat hulls and large shells because it is lightweight, corrosion resistant, resistant to seawater and can be laid into complex mould shapes. Limitations include labour/time during lay-up, resin fumes, possible voids or delamination, difficult repair and poor recyclability. fileciteturn40file5L578-L608

## Construction composites

**Concrete** is strong in compression but weak in tension. Adding steel reinforcement creates **reinforced concrete**, where the two materials complement each other: concrete carries compressive loads while steel carries tensile loads. fileciteturn40file6L692-L700

**Fibre cement** adds fibres to a cement matrix, improving toughness and allowing thin durable construction products such as roofing and façade panels. fileciteturn40file6L702-L708

**Glulam** is made from timber laminations bonded together with durable adhesive. The laminations allow defects to be distributed and large, stable structural sections to be manufactured, including curved beams and arches. fileciteturn40file6L710-L718

## Exam focus

When asked why a composite is used, identify **what each part contributes**. A strong answer sounds like: **reinforced concrete is suitable for a bridge because concrete has high compressive strength while the embedded steel reinforcement provides the tensile strength that plain concrete lacks.**
