## Polymer-based sheet and film

Polymer sheet and film are flat stock forms selected according to **thickness, flexibility, toughness, transparency, chemical resistance, barrier properties, printability and forming behaviour**.

### Sheet and film

**Sheet** is thicker and generally more rigid than film. It can be cut, line bent, machined, thermoformed or vacuum formed into products such as guards, displays, trays, packaging and housings.

**Film** is much thinner and flexible. It is used where a product needs to wrap, seal, protect or contain something with very little added mass. Film is available in different thicknesses and widths and can be printed, laminated or heat sealed depending on the polymer.

### Common stock forms from the teaching material

| Material / form | Useful characteristics | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **LDPE film** | Flexible, tough, lightweight, chemical resistant and a useful moisture barrier | Bags, wrapping and flexible packaging |
| **Cellulose acetate film** | Clear or translucent, flexible and suitable for thin film products | Transparent packaging and film applications |
| **PLA film/sheet** | Plant-derived polymer that can be biodegradable/compostable under suitable conditions | Short-life packaging and disposable products |
| **PMMA/acrylic sheet** | Rigid, transparent, weather resistant and has good optical clarity | Displays, signs, light covers and transparent guards |
| **HIPS sheet** | Tough, impact resistant, economical and easy to thermoform | Vacuum-formed trays, packaging and liners |
| **Polypropylene sheet** | Tough, lightweight and fatigue resistant; thin sections can flex repeatedly | Folders, packaging, boxes and living hinges |
| **Foam board** | Lightweight and rigid with an easily cut surface | Presentation and architectural models |
| **Styrofoam / expanded foam** | Very low density and easy to cut or shape | Models, formers and protective packaging |
| **Plastazote foam** | Tough, flexible closed-cell foam with impact absorption | Protective packaging, mats and flotation products |

### Why the stock form matters

The same broad material family can perform very differently depending on its form. A rigid transparent sheet can become a structural guard or display, while a thin flexible film is more useful for wrapping and sealing. Designers therefore select both the **polymer and the stock form**, rather than specifying only “plastic”.

### Packaging performance

Film can protect a product from **moisture, contamination and handling** while adding little mass. Some polymers also provide useful gas or moisture barrier properties and can be heat sealed for efficient packaging production.

There are trade-offs. A thicker or multilayer film may improve strength and barrier performance but use more material, and mixed or laminated films can be difficult to separate for recycling. The required protection therefore needs to be balanced against material use and end-of-life recovery.
