<section class="note-panel"><h2>Product Life Cycle</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>The <strong>product life cycle (PLC)</strong> shows how a product's sales change from launch until it is withdrawn.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Stages of the Product Life Cycle</h3><ul><li><strong>Introduction:</strong> The product is launched. Sales are usually low while customers become aware of it.</li><li><strong>Growth:</strong> Sales rise as more customers buy the product. Competitors may introduce alternatives.</li><li><strong>Maturity:</strong> Sales reach a high level and stabilise. The manufacturer tries to maintain demand.</li><li><strong>Decline:</strong> Sales fall as customer needs or technology change. The product may be withdrawn.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Extension Strategies</h3><p>An <strong>extension strategy</strong> attempts to delay decline by improving performance, changing the appearance, adding features or releasing an updated version.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Demand Pull and Technology Push</h3><p><strong>Demand pull</strong> occurs when customer needs encourage a new or improved product. <strong>Technology push</strong> occurs when a new technology enables a product that a company then introduces to the market. Technology push can fail if customers do not find the product useful or consider it too expensive.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Planned Obsolescence</h3><p><strong>Planned obsolescence</strong> is the deliberate design of a product so that it needs replacing sooner than necessary. Unavailable spare parts or loss of software compatibility can contribute to this. It can increase sales but also creates waste and costs consumers money.</p></div></section>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Evolution and redevelopment</h3><p>AQA includes <strong>evolution</strong> as part of the product life cycle. Products can be refined and redeveloped as user expectations, technology, materials and competing products change. This can involve improving an existing model rather than immediately replacing it with a completely unrelated product. Designers should be able to use a specific product example to explain how successive versions have been refined through the life cycle.</p></div>
