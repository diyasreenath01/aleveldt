## Open design

**Open design** is a collaborative approach in which design information is shared so that other people can access, use and develop it. It can be used to create products and systems for the **common good of society** rather than primarily for financial reward.

Open design encourages collaboration and sharing instead of relying entirely on conventional intellectual-property protection. Many projects are internet based, allowing digital design information to be distributed widely.

### Digital sharing

Digital manufacture makes open design particularly useful because files for processes such as **3D printing** can be shared and manufactured in different locations from the same design information. Common file formats make this easier.

Open design does not necessarily mean that the creator gives up all rights. Licences such as **Creative Commons** can specify how work may be shared, used or adapted.

### Examples

**Practical Action** shares designs for technologies intended to improve people's lives, including systems for water pumping and purification.

**Fairphone** demonstrates related ideas through open-source development, modular construction, repair and upgrading, helping extend product life.

### Advantages and limitations

Open design can:

- encourage collaboration and further development
- make useful designs accessible to more people
- support socially beneficial projects
- allow digital designs to be manufactured locally
- make replacement parts and modification easier.

However, shared designs still need appropriate **testing, quality control and clear licensing**. Easy access to a design file does not guarantee that every manufactured version will be safe or correctly made.
