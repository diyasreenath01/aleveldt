<h2>Protecting designs and intellectual property</h2>
<div class="study-card"><p>This topic is for A-level only. If you are studying at AS-level you do not need to cover the content in this chapter.</p></div>
<h2>Intellectual property (IP)</h2>
<div class="study-card"><p>• <strong>Intellectual property (IP)</strong> is an idea that is turned into a physical creation that did not already exist (for example, a book, film, camera lens mounting system, handbag design or car name badge).<br> • Ideas alone do not count as IP – physical format of some kind is compulsory.<br> • Uniqueness is essential when seeking to protect IP with <strong>intellectual property rights (IPR)</strong>.<br> • Some rights are automatically endowed when work is created (e.g. copyright); others have to be applied for (e.g. patent).<br> • IP laws vary from country to country and there are various rules governing applications.</p></div>
<h2>Copyright and design rights</h2>
<div class="study-card"><p>• <strong>Copyright</strong> is legal protection for work such as books, photos, drama, music, films, TV programmes, software, etc.<br> • It allows management of permission for others to use work (e.g. licensing photos for use in books).<br> • The UK Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 provides cover for most works for 70 years after the death of the creator.<br> • <strong>Unregistered design rights</strong> provide legal protection for designers to stop unauthorised copying for ten years. They cover appearance (shape, ornamentation, etc.), not how products work.<br> • Registration is not required, but ownership is hard to prove in a dispute.<br> • Using the copyright symbol (©), and metadata and watermarks on digital files, makes it easier to assert ownership.<br> • It is a good idea to keep early drafts, sketches, recordings, etc., as well as deliberate, unique modifications as evidence.<br> • The UK Copyright Service provides an independent evidence of authorship facility.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Typical mistake</h3>
<p>If an exam question asks you to explain how designers protect their designs from being copied, you are expected to demonstrate your knowledge of a range of IPR. Don’t therefore just write about ways of keeping work secret, as this is only one aspect of the topic, which mainly affects patent applications.</p></div>
