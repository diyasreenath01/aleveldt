<section class="note-panel"><h2>Trademarks and logos</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>• <strong>Trademarks</strong> give legal protection for brand identity and facilitate marketing. A recognisable combination of words, sounds, colours and logos is important for companies.<br> • A <strong>logo</strong> is a graphical symbol; it needs to be instantly identifiable and unique in order to stand out.<br> • A trademark may incorporate graphics, but can be a word or phrase such as ‘dual cyclone’.<br> • Trademarks are registered by making an application and paying a fee to the IPO.<br> • Trademarks need to be renewed every ten years.<br> • The ® symbol indicates a registered trade mark and should not be used for an unregistered mark. A logo is not automatically a registered trade mark; registration depends on the relevant application and rules.</p></div>
<div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Exam tip</h3>
<p>Many products encompass all the four main types of IPR, so use a specific product example that you are familiar with, such as the Bic® Atlantis® pens in Figure 24.4 to illustrate how designers can protect their work from being copied.</p>
<p>• <strong>Patent:</strong> incorporates licensed Laszlo Biro ball ink delivery system.<br> • <strong>Registered design:</strong> the ‘S’ curve, barrel shape and other aesthetic features.<br> • <strong>Trademark:</strong> the Bic® and Atlantis® names are both registered.<br> • <strong>Copyright:</strong> all text on the packaging, such as safety instructions.</p></div></div></section>

<section class="note-panel"><h2>Applying intellectual-property protection to products</h2><div class="study-card"><p>Choose protection based on <strong>what is being protected</strong>, not merely the fact that the product is new:</p><ul><li><strong>Patent:</strong> a qualifying technical invention, such as a new dispensing mechanism.</li><li><strong>Registered design:</strong> the distinctive visual appearance of the dispenser.</li><li><strong>Trade mark:</strong> a distinctive brand name or logo identifying the business.</li><li><strong>Copyright:</strong> original qualifying artwork, illustrations and text.</li></ul><p>When explaining a decision, identify the feature, name the relevant right and explain how it helps protect the designer or business. A single product may involve several rights, but they protect different aspects.</p><p>UK registered trade marks normally require renewal every ten years. Registration is not the same as automatically owning every word or shape: the mark must meet registration criteria and the protection relates to the relevant goods or services.</p></div></section>
