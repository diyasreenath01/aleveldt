# Safe working practices and PPE

Safe working protects the operator and people nearby. A **hazard** is something capable of causing harm; **risk** is the likelihood and severity of that harm. A **risk assessment** identifies hazards, who may be affected, and suitable control measures before work begins.

## Choosing the right controls

Eliminate a hazard where possible, or reduce exposure through safer processes, **machine guards**, dust/fume **extraction**, training and safe working procedures. **Personal protective equipment (PPE)** is additional protection, not a substitute for these measures.

| Task or hazard | Appropriate protection and controls | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting, drilling or machining | Eye protection, guards, secure workholding and training | Swarf or fragments can injure eyes; contact with moving cutters can cause serious injury |
| Sanding wood or composites | Suitable dust extraction and, where required by the assessment, respiratory protection and eye protection | Fine dust can be inhaled and irritate or damage the respiratory system |
| Welding or brazing | Appropriate welding eye/face protection, protective clothing, gloves and fume extraction | Intense light, heat, sparks and fumes create different hazards |
| Handling paints, solvents, adhesives or resins | Ventilation/extraction and chemically suitable gloves or eye protection where specified by safety information | Vapours and skin/eye contact may be harmful |
| Loud machinery | Hearing protection where noise cannot be sufficiently controlled at source | Prolonged exposure can damage hearing |
| Handling heavy or sharp materials | Suitable footwear, safe lifting and task-appropriate hand protection | Reduces crushing and cut injuries |

**Important:** loose sleeves, jewellery, long hair and unsuitable gloves can become caught in rotating machinery. Gloves are not automatically safe around drills or lathes. PPE must be selected for the actual machine and risk assessment.

## Legislation and responsibilities

- **Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974:** employers must protect employees and others so far as reasonably practicable, including safe equipment, training and systems of work. Employees must take reasonable care and follow safety instructions.
- **COSHH (Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002):** requires assessment and control of exposure to hazardous substances, including wood dust, fumes, solvents and some adhesives. Safety data and hazard labels help identify appropriate controls.
- **RIDDOR (Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013):** requires certain serious work-related injuries, diseases and dangerous occurrences to be reported.
- **Health and Safety Executive (HSE):** the UK regulator that provides guidance and enforces workplace health and safety law.

## Applying a risk assessment

For **cutting timber on a bandsaw**, hazards include contact with the blade, airborne wood dust and flying particles. Controls include a properly adjusted blade guard, push sticks where appropriate, trained operation, extraction, secure working practices and eye protection. The assessment should identify who is at risk, whether controls are adequate and when it needs review.

**Key distinction:** PPE reduces exposure to a remaining hazard; a guard or extraction system controls the hazard closer to its source. The strongest answer identifies a specific hazard, explains the harm it could cause and selects a control that addresses it.
