<section class="note-panel"><h2>Inclusive Design</h2><div class="study-card"><h3>Inclusive Design</h3><p><strong>Inclusive design</strong> aims to make mainstream products accessible and usable by as many people as reasonably possible.</p><p>Examples include <strong>low floor buses</strong> for wheelchair users, <strong>textured pedestrian crossings</strong> for people with sight impairments, and <strong>easy grip utensils</strong> for people with limited hand strength. These features often benefit other users too, such as people with pushchairs, luggage or temporary injuries.</p><p>Designers should identify who may be excluded, use relevant measurements and user research, and test solutions with the people who will use them.</p></div></section>
