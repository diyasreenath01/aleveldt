<section class="note-panel"><h2>Social Problems</h2><div class="study-card"><h3>Designing for Social Needs</h3><p>Design can respond to problems involving <strong>health, safety, poverty and access to resources</strong>. Examples include wind up radios that work without disposable batteries, safer alternatives to kerosene lighting, prosthetic limbs and patient specific medical implants.</p><p>A solution must be suitable for its setting. <strong>Appropriate technology</strong> should be affordable, safe, and possible to operate and maintain using available resources and skills.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Emergency Design and Migration</h3><p>People displaced by conflict or disaster may need <strong>shelter, clean water, food preparation and healthcare</strong>. Designers have developed flat pack shelters, portable water treatment systems, solar cookers and mobile medical facilities.</p><p>An emergency shelter should be assessed for <strong>transport efficiency, assembly time, weather protection, ventilation, safety and suitability for the local climate</strong>.</p></div></section>
