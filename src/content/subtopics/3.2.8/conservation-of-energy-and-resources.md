<section class="note-panel"><h2>Conserving Energy and Resources</h2><div class="note-intro"><p>Responsible design aims to reduce unnecessary use of finite resources and energy throughout a product's life.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Material and Resource Use</h3><p>Designers should consider whether a resource is finite, the energy needed to process it, transport distances, product lifespan and whether materials can be reused or recycled. Reducing packaging weight and volume, using recycled content, and designing products for repair, reuse and disassembly can conserve resources.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Renewable Energy</h3><p><strong>Renewable energy</strong> includes solar, wind and hydro power. It can reduce reliance on fossil fuels, although weather-dependent generation may require storage or a backup supply.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Circular Use of Resources</h3><p>Keeping products and materials in use for longer reduces demand for new resources. Durable, repairable and upgradeable products can extend useful life before materials need to be recovered or recycled.</p></div></section>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Waste, surplus and by-products</h3><p>Sustainable manufacturing should minimise waste and consider whether <strong>material off-cuts, chemicals, heat and water</strong> can be reused or recovered. Waste also has a financial cost because businesses may pay for unused material, handling, treatment and disposal.</p></div><div class="study-card"><h3>Product miles</h3><p><strong>Product miles</strong> describe the distance materials, components and finished products travel through a global supply chain. Designers and manufacturers should consider the environmental impact of sourcing, manufacture and transport when deciding where products and components are produced.</p></div>
