## Collaborative working

Modern products are often developed by **multidisciplinary teams**. Product designers, engineers, material specialists, manufacturing engineers, marketing staff, suppliers, clients and users can contribute different expertise to the same project.

Collaboration is useful because technical, manufacturing and user problems can be identified earlier. Manufacturing specialists may suggest simpler production methods, engineers can check performance and safety, suppliers can advise on materials and lead times, and users can reveal usability problems that designers may not notice themselves.

### Face-to-face and virtual collaboration

Teams may work together physically or use shared CAD files, cloud documents, video meetings and project-management systems. Virtual collaboration allows specialists in different locations and time zones to contribute without travelling and can speed up the exchange of design information.

Collaboration is not automatically efficient. Poor communication, incompatible file versions, unclear responsibilities or conflicting priorities can create errors and delays. Successful collaborative work therefore needs clear roles, controlled versions of drawings/specifications and regular communication.

For complex products, the value of collaboration is greatest when feedback changes decisions early enough to avoid expensive redesign or rework after production has begun.
