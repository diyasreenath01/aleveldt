## Collaborative working

Modern products are usually developed by **multidisciplinary teams** rather than a single designer. Collaboration allows different specialists to contribute knowledge during development.

A team may include product designers, engineers, material specialists, manufacturing engineers, marketing staff, finance staff, suppliers, clients and users.

### Why collaboration is useful

- specialists can identify problems outside one designer's expertise
- manufacturing staff can suggest changes that make a product easier or cheaper to produce
- engineers can check technical performance and safety
- marketing teams can contribute customer and market information
- suppliers can advise on materials, components, lead times and manufacturing capability
- users can provide feedback on usability and desirability.

### Face-to-face and virtual collaboration

Teams may work together physically or through shared CAD files, cloud documents, video meetings, project-management systems and other digital platforms. Virtual collaboration allows specialists in different locations to work on the same project and can reduce travel and speed up communication.

However, poor communication, incompatible file versions, unclear responsibility or conflicting priorities can cause mistakes and delays. Good collaboration therefore needs clear roles, shared information and controlled versions of drawings and specifications.

### Exam application

A useful chain is:

**different specialists collaborate → problems identified earlier → design modified before production → less rework/waste → lower cost and reduced commercial risk**.
