## The manufacturing process

The required **scale of production** affects how a product is manufactured.

### One-off production

A single product is made, often for a particular client. Skilled workers can use flexible tools and adjust individual components during manufacture.

### Batch production

A set number of identical products is manufactured. **Jigs, fixtures and templates** can reduce repeated marking out and improve speed, consistency and accuracy.

### Mass production

Very large quantities of standardised products are manufactured, often using dedicated tooling, automated machinery and organised production lines. Initial setup costs can be high, but unit cost can fall when large quantities are produced.

### Effect of increasing production quantity

A process suitable for a prototype or one-off product may be too slow or expensive for large quantities. Moving to batch or mass manufacture can therefore require:
- dedicated tooling, jigs or fixtures
- greater automation
- standardised components
- tighter process control
- planned quality-control checks
- changes to the product so it can be manufactured and assembled efficiently.
