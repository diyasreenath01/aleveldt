<section class="note-panel"><h2>Polymer Finishing</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>Polymers are generally considered <strong>self-finishing</strong> materials. This means that once they are removed from the mould or manufacturing process, they require no additional surface treatments to be functional or aesthetically pleasing.</p></div>
<div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Overmoulding</h3>
<p>Overmoulding involves moulding a second polymer (often <strong>TPE - Thermoplastic Elastomer</strong>) over specific parts of a base product to provide grip, texture, or color contrast.</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th><p><strong>Method</strong></p></th><th><p><strong>Process Description</strong></p></th><th><p><strong>Best For...</strong></p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p><strong>Two-Mould Process</strong></p></td><td><p>The main product is injection moulded in one machine. It is then manually or robotically moved to a <strong>second mould</strong> where the overmoulding polymer is injected onto it.</p></td><td><p>Lower volume production or complex parts.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Twin-Shot Injection</strong></p></td><td><p>A high-speed, continuous process. The machine uses a <strong>rotating mould (180°)</strong> with two cavities. The main body is shot, the mould rotates, and the overmould is shot immediately.</p></td><td><p>High-volume mass production (e.g., toothbrushes, power tool grips).</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Acrylic Spray Paints (A-level Only)</h3>
<p>While polymers are self-finishing, acrylic paints are sometimes applied for specific functional or aesthetic reasons.</p>
<p><strong>Properties:</strong> Fast-drying, water-soluble when liquid, but <strong>water-resistant</strong> and UV-resistant once dry.</p>
<p><strong>Protection:</strong> Provides a barrier against UV light and weathering, which can prevent polymers from becoming brittle.</p>
<p><strong>Mass Customisation:</strong> Used extensively in the <strong>automotive industry</strong> (e.g., wing mirrors and bumpers).</p>
<p><strong>Efficiency:</strong> It is more cost-effective to spray-paint small batches of different colors than to stop an injection moulding machine to change the color of the polymer granules (which causes downtime and waste).</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Adding Pigments</h3>
<p>Pigments are the primary method for coloring polymers. They are integrated into the material rather than sitting on the surface.</p>
<p><strong>Application:</strong> Pigments can be added during the initial manufacture of the polymer <strong>stock form</strong> or mixed into the granules during the injection moulding/extrusion process.</p>
<p><strong>Smart Pigments:</strong></p>
<p><strong>Thermochromic:</strong> Changes color based on temperature (e.g., baby spoons).</p>
<p><strong>Phosphorescent:</strong> Glows in the dark (e.g., emergency exit signs).</p>
<p><strong>GRP (Glass Reinforced Plastic):</strong> Pigments are added to the <strong>gel coat</strong> (the outer layer) during the lay-up process to provide a high-quality color finish.</p>
<p><strong>Functional Coding:</strong> Using colors to categorize products, such as color-coded kitchen chopping boards to prevent cross-contamination</p></div></div></section>
