<section class="note-panel"><h2>Metal Finishing</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>Most metals naturally form an oxide layer when exposed to oxygen. However, the <strong>effectiveness</strong> of this layer as a barrier varies significantly between metals.</p>
<p><strong>Copper:</strong> Forms a green <strong>patina</strong>. This layer is stable and protective, preventing further corrosion of the metal underneath.</p>
<p><strong>Mild Steel:</strong> Forms a <strong>porous</strong> oxide layer (rust). Because it is porous, moisture and oxygen can penetrate the layer, causing the metal to continue corroding until it fails.</p>
<p><strong>Stainless Steel:</strong> Forms a <strong>stable, non-porous</strong> chromium oxide layer. This creates an airtight seal that prevents any further corrosion.</p>
<p><strong>Exam Tip:</strong> Never refer to a paint finish simply as a “spray.” <strong>Spraying</strong> is the <em>method</em> of application; the <strong>finish</strong> is the paint itself (e.g., cellulose or acrylic).</p>

<section class="note-panel"><h2>Cathodic Protection</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>Cathodic protection is an electrochemical technique used to prevent corrosion. When two different metals are in contact in the presence of moisture, they form an electrochemical cell. The more reactive metal becomes the <strong>anode</strong> (and corrodes), while the less reactive metal becomes the <strong>cathode</strong> (and is protected).</p></div>
<div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Methods of Protection</h3>
<p><strong>Impressed Current</strong></p>
<p>An external power supply provides a continuous flow of electricity to the structure.</p>
<p><strong>Usage:</strong> Large-scale infrastructure like underground pipelines and storage tanks.</p>
<p><strong>Sacrificial Anodes</strong></p>
<p>A block of a <strong>more reactive metal</strong> (like zinc or magnesium) is attached to the main structure.</p>
<p>The sacrificial metal corrodes away entirely to save the main structure.</p>
<p><strong>Usage:</strong> Ship hulls, offshore oil rigs, and underground pylons.</p></div></div></section>
