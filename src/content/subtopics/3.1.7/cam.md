<div class="study-card"><h3>Computer aided manufacture (CAM)</h3>
<p>CAM uses digital design data to control manufacturing machinery, most commonly CNC machines.</p>
<p><strong>CAM processes</strong></p>
<p>CAD files are converted into CNC control programs</p>
<p>Machine parameters such as feed rate, cutter speed, and tool paths are set automatically</p>
<p>Enables efficient one-off and small-batch production</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>CNC manufacturing processes</h3>
<p><strong>Laser cutting</strong></p>
<p>Uses high-energy laser beams to cut or engrave materials</p>
<p>Compressed gas removes waste to produce clean edges</p>
<p>Operates mainly on X and Y axes</p>
<p><strong>Routing</strong></p>
<p>High-speed cutters machine sheet materials such as MDF</p>
<p>Can machine simple 3D forms using X, Y, and Z axes</p>
<p>Workpiece is fixed to the machine bed</p>
<p><strong>Milling</strong></p>
<p>More rigid machines suitable for metal cutting</p>
<p>Multiple axes allow complex surfaces and features</p>
<p>Enclosed for safety; coolant improves tool life and surface finish</p>
<p><strong>Turning</strong></p>
<p>Produces cylindrical parts from bar stock</p>
<p>Automatically changes tools, often using tungsten carbide tips</p>
<p>Commonly integrated into flexible manufacturing systems (FMS)</p>
<p><strong>Plotter cutting</strong></p>
<p>Cuts compliant materials such as vinyl</p>
<p>Uses X-Y coordinate paths from 2D CAD files</p>
<p>Commonly used for graphics and signage</p></div>
