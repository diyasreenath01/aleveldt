<div class="note-grid"><div class="study-card"><h3>Computer aided manufacture (CAM)</h3>
<p>CAM uses digital design data to control manufacturing machinery, most commonly CNC machines.</p>
<p>CAD files are converted into machine toolpaths or control programs. Parameters such as feed rate, cutter speed and tool movement can then be set for manufacture.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>DXF in CAD/CAM</h3>
<p><strong>DXF (Drawing Exchange Format)</strong> stores 2D vector geometry. It is commonly used to transfer outlines and profiles from CAD to <strong>laser cutters, CNC routers and plotters</strong>.</p>
<p>Using a standard digital format reduces the need to redraw geometry when moving between compatible CAD and CAM systems.</p></div>
<div class="note-group-title"><h3>CNC manufacturing processes</h3></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Laser cutting</h4>
<p>Uses a high-energy laser beam to cut or engrave materials</p>
<p>Compressed gas can remove waste and help produce clean edges</p>
<p>Operates mainly on X and Y axes</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Routing</h4>
<p>High-speed cutters machine sheet materials such as MDF</p>
<p>Can machine simple 3D forms using X, Y and Z axes</p>
<p>Workpiece is fixed to the machine bed</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Milling</h4>
<p>Rigid CNC machines can cut metals and other engineering materials</p>
<p>Multiple axes allow complex surfaces, slots, holes and profiles to be machined accurately</p>
<p>Enclosed machines and coolant can improve safety, tool life and surface finish</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Turning</h4>
<p>Produces cylindrical parts from bar stock</p>
<p>CNC lathes can change tools automatically and repeat the same programmed geometry accurately</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h4>Plotter cutting</h4>
<p>Cuts compliant materials such as vinyl</p>
<p>Uses X-Y coordinate paths from 2D CAD files</p>
<p>Commonly used for graphics and signage</p></div></div></section>
