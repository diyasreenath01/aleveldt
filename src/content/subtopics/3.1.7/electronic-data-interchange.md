## Electronic data interchange

In this part of the AQA specification, electronic data interchange is mainly about how **EPOS (Electronic Point of Sale)** data can be collected and shared digitally to support marketing, stock control and decision-making.

### EPOS

When a product is scanned at the point of sale, the system can record information such as the product sold, quantity, time and location of the sale. If linked to a loyalty or customer account, it may also provide useful customer data.

This information can be used to:
- monitor sales and identify buying trends
- maintain stock levels and trigger replenishment
- identify popular and slow-selling products
- collect market-research data
- support targeted marketing and promotions
- respond more quickly to changes in consumer demand.

### Why this matters to designers and manufacturers

Sales data can influence future product development. For example, if one product variant consistently sells better than another, a business may change its product range, production quantities, features or marketing.

Digital information can also be shared quickly between retailers, warehouses, manufacturers and suppliers, reducing delays and helping coordinate stock and production.

### Useful evaluation point

EPOS data gives businesses **real purchasing evidence**, but the data still needs interpretation. It shows what customers bought, not necessarily why they chose it. Designers may therefore combine sales data with interviews, questionnaires, reviews or focus groups.
