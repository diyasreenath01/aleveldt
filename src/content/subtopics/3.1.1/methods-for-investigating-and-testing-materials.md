<section class="note-panel"><h2>Methods for investigating and testing materials</h2>
<div class="note-intro"><p>Workshop tests should allow materials to be compared under controlled conditions. A fair test keeps relevant variables consistent and measures the result objectively.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Flexural test: malleability and ductility</h3><ul><li>A bending test can use a mandrel pushing upwards and a plunger pushing downwards.</li><li>The uploaded workshop notes tested acrylic sheet, metal sheet and wood.</li><li>For a fairer comparison, specimens should be held consistently, tested in the same environment and subjected to a measured, repeatable load.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Hardness test</h3><ul><li>The workshop method uses a centre punch to compare resistance to indentation.</li><li>Keep the centre-punch diameter, specimen size, support, impact method and angle consistent.</li><li>A different hammer, angle or applied force would reduce the reliability of the comparison.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Toughness test</h3><ul><li>The workshop test uses a pendulum impact arrangement.</li><li>Keep specimen sizes, pendulum/string length and starting position consistent.</li><li>Release rather than push the pendulum so no extra force is added.</li><li>Measurement error can occur if the protractor is not positioned correctly.</li></ul></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Tensile strength test</h3><p>The uploaded notes show a simple workshop tensile test and emphasise keeping the test arrangement and specimen position consistent.</p></div>
<div class="study-card"><h3>Coverage warning</h3><p>These workshop notes provide useful material for tensile strength, toughness, hardness and malleability, but they do not adequately cover corrosion or conductivity. This page therefore does not yet represent complete specification coverage.</p></div></section>
