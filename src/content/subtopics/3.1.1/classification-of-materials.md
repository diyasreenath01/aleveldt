# Classification of materials

AQA expects you to know the main **material classifications** and be able to name examples belonging to each. Classification is about the material family; in an exam you may then need to use the material's properties to justify why it suits a product.

## Metals

### Ferrous metals

Ferrous metals are iron-based metals/alloys. Many are magnetic and most are susceptible to corrosion unless alloyed or protected.

Examples you should recognise include **low-carbon steel (mild steel), medium-carbon steel, cast iron, high-speed steel (HSS) and stainless steel**. Stainless steel is iron-based, so it is classed as ferrous even though its chromium content gives it excellent corrosion resistance.

### Non-ferrous metals

Non-ferrous metals do not have iron as their principal constituent. AQA examples include **aluminium, copper, zinc, silver, gold, titanium and tin**.

### Alloys

An **alloy** is a mixture of a metal with one or more other elements to obtain improved or altered properties.

- **Ferrous alloys:** stainless steel and tool/die steels.
- **Non-ferrous alloys:** **bronze, brass, duralumin and pewter**.

A useful distinction is that **ferrous/non-ferrous tells you whether the base metal is iron**, whereas **alloy tells you the material contains a controlled mixture of elements**. A metal can therefore be both *ferrous* and an *alloy* — stainless steel is the obvious example.

## Woods

### Hardwoods

Hardwoods come from **deciduous broad-leaved trees** (angiosperms). They are not automatically harder than every softwood. Examples: **oak, ash, mahogany, teak, birch and beech**.

### Softwoods

Softwoods generally come from **coniferous evergreen trees** (gymnosperms). Examples: **pine, spruce, Douglas fir, redwood, cedar and larch**.

### Manufactured boards

Manufactured boards are produced by bonding wood veneers, fibres, particles or other wood material, often with resin. Examples include **plywood, marine plywood, aeroply, flexible plywood, chipboard and MDF**.

## Polymers

### Thermoplastics

Soften when heated and harden when cooled, allowing repeated reshaping. Examples: **LDPE, HDPE, PP, HIPS, ABS, PMMA/acrylic, nylon, PVC and PET**.

### Thermosetting polymers

Form permanent cross-links during curing and therefore cannot simply be reheated and remoulded. Examples: **urea formaldehyde, melamine formaldehyde, polyester resin and epoxy resin**.

### Elastomers

Polymers capable of considerable elastic deformation and returning towards their original shape. AQA examples include **natural rubber, polybutadiene, neoprene, silicone and TPE**.

## Papers and boards

Papers and boards are cellulose-fibre sheet materials. Examples include **layout paper, cartridge paper, tracing paper, bleed-proof paper, watercolour paper, corrugated card, bleached card, mount board, duplex card and laminated/foil-backed board**.

## Composites

A **composite** combines two or more materials so their properties work together to improve performance. Examples include **CFRP, GRP, tungsten carbide, aluminium composite board, concrete/reinforced concrete, fibre cement and glulam**.

## Smart materials

A **smart material** changes one or more properties in response to an external stimulus such as **temperature, light or pressure/force**. AQA examples include **shape-memory alloy (Nitinol), thermochromic pigment, phosphorescent pigment, photochromic pigment, electroluminescent wire and piezoelectric material**.

## Modern materials

AQA uses **modern materials** for relatively recently developed/advanced materials that offer properties or processing possibilities not associated with traditional materials. Named examples include **Kevlar, precious metal clay (PMC), high-density modelling foam and polymorph**.

## Exam focus

Do not confuse **classification** with **property**. “Aluminium is non-ferrous” is a classification. “Aluminium has low density and good corrosion resistance” describes properties. A strong answer can use both: **aluminium is a non-ferrous metal with low density and good corrosion resistance, making it suitable for lightweight outdoor products.**
