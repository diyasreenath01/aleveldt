## The central idea

The Arts and Crafts movement developed in Britain in the late nineteenth century. It valued skilled making, useful objects and honesty about materials. Its supporters responded to the poor quality and harsh working conditions they associated with some industrial production.

## People and influences

**William Morris** was a central figure. He argued that everyday surroundings should be beautiful and that making should be meaningful for the craftsperson. His wallpapers and textiles often use repeating natural forms such as leaves and flowers.

The movement drew inspiration from medieval craft traditions, where makers controlled the design and production of an object. This shaped a preference for visible construction and careful workmanship.

## Recognising the style

- Natural motifs, especially plants and flowers.
- Handcrafted details and visible material qualities.
- Useful forms that bring beauty into everyday life.
- Patterns with rhythm and repetition.

## Exam connection

When discussing the movement, connect a **design choice** to its **reason** and **effect**. For example, a floral pattern reflects interest in nature and can make an everyday interior feel more considered. Avoid saying that every Arts and Crafts product was handmade: describe a specific object and its production when evidence is available.

> **Remember:** context → design feature → intended effect makes a stronger answer than a list of style words.
