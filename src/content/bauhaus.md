## The central idea

The Bauhaus was a German school of art, craft and design founded by Walter Gropius in 1919. It brought different disciplines together and explored how design could meet the needs of modern life.

## Form, function and production

Students learned through making and experimentation. The school increasingly explored how simple forms and new production methods could help create useful objects for a wider public. Geometric shapes, reduced decoration and a clear relationship between form and function became associated with its work.

**Marcel Breuer** experimented with tubular steel furniture. The material enabled visually light structures and suggested new ways to manufacture furniture. **Marianne Brandt** designed metalwork, including lamps and household objects, with careful attention to geometry and use.

## Recognising the approach

- Simple geometric shapes and purposeful composition.
- Experimentation with modern materials and manufacturing.
- A link between art, craft and industry.
- Design decisions justified by use, construction and production.

## Exam connection

Avoid treating “form follows function” as a complete explanation. Name a feature, explain what it does and link it to the designer's aim. For a tubular steel chair, for example, discuss how the structure changes the chair's weight, appearance or production.

> **Remember:** Bauhaus work varied across its history. Support broad claims with a named designer and object.
